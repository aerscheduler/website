<Summary>
Start a booking, set the type to **Maintenance**, pick the aircraft and the window, and
save. Nobody is assigned to it, it blocks the whole window on the dispatch board, and it is
never invoiced.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins, Dispatchers, Technicians</Roles>

## Make the booking

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Start a new booking">
    From <Where app="web">Schedule</Where>, or from the app. A technician-only account goes
    straight there: the call to action reads "Schedule maintenance" rather than "Book a
    flight", and the Type is already fixed to Maintenance.

    <Screenshot id="me-book-maintenance" />
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Set the type to Maintenance">
    If you hold another role as well, pick it from the Type list.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Pick the aircraft and the window">
    Book the whole time the aircraft will be unavailable, including the ferry out and back
    if it is going to another field.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Put what is being done in the notes">
    There is nobody to assign, so the notes are the only place the shift on the other end
    can read what this block is for.
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="Save">
    The aircraft is blocked for the whole window on the dispatch board.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Nobody can be assigned to it

A maintenance booking carries no people by design. Try to add one and it is refused: a
maintenance booking cannot have anybody assigned to it, because it takes the aircraft off
the line rather than putting somebody in it.

## It works on a grounded aircraft

Maintenance is the one booking type a grounded aircraft accepts. Every other type is refused
with "Resource is grounded."

<Screenshot id="booking-airworthiness-notice" />

That exemption holds all the way through: creating the booking, editing it, dragging it on
the dispatch board, and ramping it out. It exists because these bookings are how a grounded
aircraft gets to the shop and back.

## It is never invoiced

Closing out a maintenance booking never produces an invoice, whatever rates the aircraft
carries. Shop time is not a rental.

## Only the person who booked it sees it on their own calendar

Personal calendars are built from who is assigned to a booking, and a maintenance booking has
nobody assigned. The person who created it sees it on their own calendar as a special case.

<Callout type="warning">
A second technician covering the shift will not see it on their calendar at all. They have
to look at the dispatch board, where it blocks the lane in the normal way, or at the
aircraft's own schedule.
</Callout>

## Which roles can book maintenance

| Role | Can book maintenance? |
|---|---|
| Owner, Admin | Yes |
| Dispatcher | Yes |
| Technician | Yes, and it is the only type they can book |
| Instructor, Student, Renter | No |

## Aircraft only, not simulators or classrooms

A maintenance booking is refused on a simulator or a room. To keep a sim or a classroom
clear while it is being worked on, block the window with an ordinary booking instead.

<Callout type="tip">
Booking maintenance is not the same as grounding. A booking blocks one window. Grounding
stops every booking on the tail until an admin lifts it. If the aircraft is not airworthy,
do both: ground it, then book the shop time. See
[Ground an aircraft](/docs/maintenance/ground-an-aircraft).
</Callout>
