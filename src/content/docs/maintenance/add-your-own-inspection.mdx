## Recurring or one-off

The dialog opens on **Standard set**, the AVIATES presets. The other two modes are for
everything else your shop tracks.

| Mode | Use it for | Comes back after sign-off? |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring | Oil changes, 50-hour checks, gear inspections, anything on a clock | Yes, immediately |
| One-off | "Prop back from the shop", a single ferry permit expiry | No |

## Add a recurring inspection

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Maintenance, then Add inspections, then Recurring">
    The mode buttons sit at the top of the dialog.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Type a name">
    Up to 60 characters. Name it the way your shop says it out loud: "Oil change",
    "50-hour", "Gear rig check".
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Choose On the calendar or On the meter">
    **On the calendar** is preselected. Switch to **On the meter** for anything counted in
    flying hours.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Fill in Every, its unit, and Warn me">
    On the calendar, pick **days**, **weeks** or **calendar months** beside the number. It
    opens on "Every 12 calendar months, warn 30 days out", which is an annual. On the meter it
    is "Every 100 hours, warn 10 hours out".

    The warning lead is always in days on the calendar side, whichever unit the interval uses.
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="On the meter, pick the meter">
    **Count tach time** or **Count hobbs time**. Tach is the default, and it is what every
    hour-based standard preset uses.
  </Step>
  <Step n={6} title="Decide whether it grounds the aircraft">
    Switch on **Grounds the aircraft** only if the tail should come off the line the moment
    this comes due.

  </Step>
  <Step n={7} title="Fill in When was it last done?">
    The date and meter reading from the last time the work was actually performed. Leave
    both blank only if it was just done.
  </Step>
  <Step n={8} title="Pick the tails and click Add">
    **Applies to** is a multi-select. **Add** stays disabled until at least one tail is

## On the calendar or on the meter

An inspection counted on the calendar recurs from the last sign-off and shows a due date. An
inspection counted on the meter counts flying hours and shows a meter figure instead: "Due at
1250.0 tach, now 1237.6."

They never convert into each other, so pick the one your shop actually works to. An oil change
measured in hours should be on the meter even though people talk about it in months.

## Days, weeks, or calendar months

**Calendar months are not a number of days, and this is the setting most worth getting right.**

A calendar month runs to the END of the month. 14 CFR 91.409(a) requires an annual "within the
preceding 12 calendar months", so an annual signed on 15 February 2026 is good through 28
February 2027, not through 15 February 2027. The transponder check (91.413), the static system
(91.411) and the ELT (91.207(d)) are all written the same way, and so are a great many recurring
Airworthiness Directives.

If you set one of those as "365 days" it will come due up to a month early, depending on which
day of which month the work was signed. That will not make you unairworthy, but it will take an
aeroplane off your line while it is still perfectly legal to fly, every year, on every tail.

- **Calendar months** for anything a regulation writes in months. Due date lands on the last day
  of the month, and it is worked out on your school's time zone.
- **Weeks** for a shop rhythm. A week is exactly seven days, so this is simply a tidier way to
  say the same thing, and "every 2 weeks" reads back as weeks.
- **Days** for anything genuinely counted in days, like the 30-day VOR check.

<Callout type="tip">
Already have an annual set to 365 days? Nothing was converted for you, because a 365-day
interval might genuinely mean 365 days and converting one moves its due date LATER, which is
not a change anybody should make on your behalf. Edit the inspection and switch its unit to
calendar months when you are ready.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
The progress bar under an inspection is how full the interval is, not how much time has
passed. On an hour-based item it moves when the aircraft flies, and sits perfectly still
during a month it does not.
</Callout>

## Set the warning lead

**Warn me** is not decoration. It is exactly when the row turns to **Due soon** and exactly
when the warning email and push are sent to your admins and technicians.

The form never lets the lead reach zero, so you cannot create an inspection through it that
comes due with no warning at all. A calendar lead cannot go below 1 day, and a meter lead
cannot go below 0.1 hours.

## Should it ground the aircraft?

**Grounds the aircraft** is the highest-consequence control on the form. With it on, the
moment this inspection comes due AerScheduler grounds that tail with the reason
"Maintenance", and nobody can book it for anything except maintenance until it is signed
off.

<Callout type="warning">
The switch starts **off** on the console and **on** in the app's Add inspection sheet.
Check it before you save, especially on the phone: an oil change that grounds an aircraft
will take a trainer off the line in the middle of a Saturday.
</Callout>

## Add a one-off dated reminder

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Maintenance, then Add inspections, then One-off">
    A single date that does not come back.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Name it">
    "Prop back from the shop", "Ferry permit expires".
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Pick Due on and Warn me">
    **Due on** must be today or later. **Warn me (days out)** starts at 10 and is clamped
    to between 1 and 30 days.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Pick the tails and click Add">
    The rule is filed under <Where app="web">Maintenance → Set up</Where> in the **One-off**
    group, with a One-off badge.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What a one-off does not do

- It never repeats. Signing it off does not create a replacement.
- It has no **When was it last done?** box, because there is nothing to count from.
- Once it is signed off on every tail it covers, the rule disappears from the Set up list entirely. That is expected, not a deletion.

## Adding one on the phone

The app's **Add inspection** button on the Maintenance page creates the rule first and then
takes you on to pick the aircraft.

<Callout type="warning">
Stop at that second step and you are left with a rule attached to nothing. It looks
identical to a working rule in the Set up list except for one line: "Not on any aircraft,
so it never comes due." Nothing will ever fire from it. Attach the tails from
<Where app="mobile">Maintenance → Set up → the rule → edit which aircraft</Where>.
</Callout>
