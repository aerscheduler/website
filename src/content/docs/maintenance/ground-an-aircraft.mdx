  <Step n={2} title="Click Ground">

## Write a reason people can act on

The reason is not a private note. It is shown to dispatchers, instructors and students
wherever the tail appears, including in the airworthiness notice on the booking form and in
the refusal message when somebody tries to drag a flight onto it.

"Oil leak, in the shop Thursday" saves your front desk five phone calls. Grounding with no
reason at all shows as "Grounded: No reason recorded."

<Callout type="important">
The reason also decides whether AerScheduler may ever lift the grounding by itself. Only the
exact automatic reason "Maintenance" is cleared automatically. Because you typed this one by
hand, signing inspections off will not lift it, deliberately. You return the aircraft to
service yourself when the work is done.
</Callout>

## What grounding stops

| Action on a grounded aircraft | Result |
|---|---|
| Creating any booking except maintenance | Refused with "Resource is grounded." |
| Creating a maintenance booking | Allowed. This is the one exemption. |
| Dragging a booking onto it on the dispatch board | Refused, naming the tail and the reason. |
| Ramping out an existing booking | Refused. |

## Bookings already on the aircraft stay where they are

<Callout type="warning">
Grounding does not cancel anything. The flights already on that tail stay on the dispatch
board, looking normal, and none of the people on them are notified. They fail at ramp out,
when the student is already standing at the desk. After you ground an aircraft, work the
board yourself and move or cancel what is on it.
</Callout>

## Return it to service

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Click Return to service">
    On the aircraft's page, or from the overflow menu on the Aircraft list. Admins and
    owners only.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Confirm">
    The dialog asks to return the tail to service and warns that it will be schedulable
    again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The grounded flag and the reason are both cleared, and a "Returned to service" line goes
into the audit log with your name on it.

## Why signing off an inspection did not lift your grounding

Because you typed the reason. AerScheduler clears a grounding on its own in exactly one
case: the reason is the single word "Maintenance", which only its own automatic grounding
ever writes, and the last inspection holding it has been signed off.

Anything a person typed is treated as a human decision and left alone. See
[When AerScheduler grounds an aircraft by itself](/docs/maintenance/when-aerscheduler-grounds-an-aircraft).

## Technicians cannot ground an aircraft

Grounding and returning to service are admin and owner actions. A technician can add and
sign off inspections, resolve squawks and book maintenance, but the **Ground** button is not
shown to them at all.

A technician who needs a tail off the line right now has two routes: ask an admin, or book
it in for maintenance, which blocks the aircraft for that window. See
[Who can do what in maintenance](/docs/maintenance/who-can-do-what-in-maintenance).
