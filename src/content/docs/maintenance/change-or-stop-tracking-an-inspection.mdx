<Summary>
To put an inspection on one more tail, open that aircraft and use
<Where app="web">Maintenance → Add</Where>. To rework the whole list at once, use the
aircraft button on the rule under <Where app="web">Maintenance → Set up</Where>. To stop
tracking something altogether, delete the rule, which stops tracking it on every aircraft it
covers.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins, Technicians</Roles>

## Add one aircraft to an inspection

From the console, work from the aircraft rather than from the rule.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open the aircraft">
    <Where app="web">Aircraft → the tail → Maintenance</Where>.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Click Add on the Inspections card">
    The Add inspections dialog opens with this tail already fixed, so there is no
    **Applies to** list to get wrong.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Set it up and fill in When was it last done?">
    This tail has its own history and its own meter. Enter the date and reading from the
    last time the work was performed on **this** aircraft. See
    [Set when an inspection was last done](/docs/maintenance/set-when-an-inspection-was-last-done).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Change the whole list at once

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open Maintenance, then Set up">
    Find the rule. Rules are grouped into On the meter, On the calendar and One-off.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Click the aircraft button on the row">
    It is labelled **Which aircraft this covers**. Tail numbers are chips: click one to
    attach it, click an attached one to detach it.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Set a starting point for any tail you just added">
    A **When was it last done?** box appears per newly added tail, for a meter reading or a
    date depending on how the rule counts. Leave it blank and that tail starts today. See
    [Set when an inspection was last done](/docs/maintenance/set-when-an-inspection-was-last-done).
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Click Save coverage">
    Removals are named on screen before you save, and confirmed again after.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The same sheet is on the app, at
<Where app="mobile">Maintenance → Set up → the rule</Where>.


## Editing the list replaces it, it does not add to it

<Callout type="warning">
The list you save becomes the complete list of aircraft on that rule. Any tail you leave
unticked is detached, and its open inspection is deleted. This looks like an ordinary
multi-select you are adding to, and it is not. Check every tail that should stay before you
save.
</Callout>

## Removing a tail deletes its open inspection

Detaching an aircraft removes the outstanding countdown for that tail and nothing else.
Everything already signed off stays on the aircraft's record and in the audit log.

If that open inspection was the last thing holding an automatic grounding on that aircraft,
the aircraft is returned to service at the same time.

## Stop tracking an inspection entirely

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open Maintenance, then Set up">
    Rules are grouped into On the meter, On the calendar and One-off.

    <Screenshot id="maintenance-set-up" />
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Expand the aircraft chips first">
    Click the "N aircraft" chip on the row and read the list. This is the one number that
    tells you how big the delete is.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Click the trash icon and read the confirmation">
    It reads "This stops tracking it on N aircraft. Work already signed off stays on the
    record."
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Click Delete">
    The rule and every outstanding inspection under it are removed, across the whole fleet.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
This is the biggest-consequence button on the page. One rule commonly covers every tail you
own, so a single delete can stop maintenance tracking on the entire fleet. The count in the
confirmation is easy to skim past.
</Callout>

## What deleting keeps

- Every signed-off inspection stays on the aircraft's record.
- One "Inspection removed" line is written to the audit log per affected aircraft, so you can see exactly what stopped being tracked and when.
- Any aircraft that this rule had grounded automatically, with nothing else outstanding, is returned to service.

## A rule on no aircraft never comes due

A rule with no tails attached looks completely normal in the Set up list except for one
line: "Not on any aircraft, so it never comes due." Nothing will ever fire from it and
nobody will ever be emailed about it.

The usual way to end up with one is on the phone, where **Add inspection** creates the rule
first and then sends you on to pick the aircraft. Abandon that second step and the rule
survives with no tails. Attach them with the aircraft button on the row, as above.
