## On your own aircraft, only admins and technicians are notified

On your organization's own aircraft there is no way to send maintenance reminders to anybody
else. Dispatchers, instructors, students and renters are never emailed or pushed about an
inspection or a squawk, whatever their notification settings say. A customer's aircraft is
different, below: its owners are reminded whatever their role.

If the person who needs to know about the fleet is not getting anything, the fix is their
role, not their preferences.

## Customer aircraft are different

An aircraft in for maintenance (one your shop looks after for an owner) works differently:

- **Technicians choose which ones they hear about**, under **Customer aircraft inspections** in
  their notification settings: **Aircraft I've worked on** (the default: aircraft you were
  assigned to on a past job), **All customer aircraft**, or **Off**. An aircraft nobody here has
  worked on yet goes to everybody whose setting is not **Off**, so nothing falls through.
- **Admins hear nothing about customer aircraft** unless they pick one of those settings
  themselves. The exception: an organization with no technicians signed in sends these to its
  admins, so somebody always hears, and their setting shows **All customer aircraft** until they
  change it.
- **The aircraft's owners are reminded too**: by default 30 days and 7 days before an inspection
  is due, when it is due, and when it is overdue; for one counted in hours, 10 hours before and
  when it is due. You can change those steps on the inspection, or for one aircraft (see
  **Choose when reminders go out**, below). One message lists everything that crossed a step at once, the most urgent first, and
  an inspection counted both ways ("100 hours or 12 months") gives both figures, whichever comes
  first. An owner who has not signed up is emailed at the address on the aircraft. Calendar
  steps go out in the working day where the organization is.
- **Owners are not reminded about what they already know.** Nothing goes out while the
  inspection is on a job the owner has been told about (booked in or further), nor for a week
  after that job is closed out as completed, while the sign-off is entered. On a job that is only
  requested, the reminder still goes, and names the job instead of asking them to call. When the
  aircraft is at the shop on another job, the reminder says so and asks them to have you add the
  inspection to it. A cancelled job hands the inspection back at once.
- **Nothing goes out about a job held from the owner.** While an inspection is on a job whose
  owner emails are on hold, its reminder waits, still owed, and follows the rules above once you
  lift the hold.
- **Somebody who owns a customer aircraft and works here** gets the due and overdue notices once,
  from the shop side by their own setting, and the earlier owner reminders as an owner.

How each person hears (email, push, or both) follows their **Maintenance reminders** switch; an
owner's is called **Inspection reminders**, under **Your aircraft**. An owner who has not signed up
is reached by email only.

## Squawk alerts cannot be turned off

Every squawk filed sends an email and a push to every admin and technician in the organization,
except the person who filed it. There is no switch for it on either surface.

<Callout type="note">
On a busy fleet this is the noisiest thing in the module. If it is too much, the practical
lever is the role: an admin who does not want squawk email and does not need admin rights
can hold a narrower role instead.
</Callout>

## When a reminder is actually sent

Each inspection warns the shop at each of its **reminder steps**, tells everybody when it comes
due, and tells the shop once more if it is still not signed off a day later.

An inspection starts with one step: the warning lead you set when you added it ("Warn me 10
hours out" warns at 10 hours remaining). Add more steps to warn earlier and again closer in, for
example 90, 30 and 7 days before an annual, or 25, 10 and 5 hours before a 100-hour.

Each step goes once per cycle, and only the nearest one: an inspection first noticed 5 days out
sends the 7-day reminder, not the 90, 30 and 7 at once. A step further out than one already sent
is never sent after it. Signing the inspection off starts the steps again for the next cycle.

An inspection with no steps sends no early warning, only the due notification.

## Choose when reminders go out

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="For every aircraft an inspection covers">
    On <Where app="web">Maintenance → Inspection rules</Where>, open the inspection's menu and
    choose **Edit name, source and reminders**. Under **Reminders**, add or remove steps for the
    shop and for the owners of customer aircraft, in days before it is due and, for one counted
    in hours, hours before. A list you have not changed shows its default and follows it.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="For one aircraft">
    Open the inspection on that aircraft and click the pencil on **Reminders**. Steps set here
    apply to this aircraft only and carry on to the next cycle when it is signed off. **Use the
    rule's** puts a list back on the inspection's.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Hour-based inspections are checked when a reading is recorded

Calendar inspections are checked automatically through the day, so a 12-month annual will
warn you on its own.

Hour-based inspections move only when the aircraft's meters do. Their notification and their
automatic grounding are checked each time a new Hobbs or tach reading reaches the aircraft:

- a flight closed out on that aircraft;
- **Record a reading** under **Hobbs and tach** on the aircraft's Overview;
- a work order's Hobbs and tach in or out;
- an inspection signed off with meter readings newer than the aircraft's;
- on a customer's aircraft, the owner's **Update times**, or the times they give with a request.

A reading dated before the newest one on record is kept as history and checks nothing.

<Callout type="warning">
A tach corrected by hand under <Where app="web">Aircraft → the tail → Edit</Where> moves the
countdown on screen but checks nothing, so the aircraft can sit at Overdue with nobody emailed and
still bookable. Record the reading with **Record a reading** instead, or the next close-out on that
tail brings everything back into line. A customer's aircraft that flies somewhere else is only as
current as its last reading. See
[Troubleshooting inspections and squawks](/docs/maintenance/troubleshooting-inspections-and-squawks).
</Callout>

## Where the email link takes you

A maintenance reminder links straight to the inspection it is about, which opens properly in
the iOS app. An owner's reminder opens their aircraft, or their **Home** when it lists more than
one aircraft.

A squawk alert's button lands on that squawk's own page, on both surfaces. Searching its
title from anywhere in the console opens the same page.
