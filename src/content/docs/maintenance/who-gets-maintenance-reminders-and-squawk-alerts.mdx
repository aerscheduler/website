## On your own aircraft, only admins and technicians are notified

On your organization's own aircraft there is no way to send maintenance reminders to anybody
else. Dispatchers, instructors, students and renters are never emailed or pushed about an
inspection or a squawk, whatever their notification settings say. A customer's aircraft is
different, below: its owners are reminded whatever their role.

If the person who needs to know about the fleet is not getting anything, the fix is their
role, not their preferences.

## Customer aircraft are different

An aircraft in for maintenance (one your shop looks after for an owner) works differently:

- **Technicians choose which ones they hear about**, under **Customer aircraft inspections** in
  their notification settings: **Aircraft I've worked on** (the default: aircraft you were
  assigned to on a past job), **All customer aircraft**, or **Off**. An aircraft nobody here has
  worked on yet goes to everybody whose setting is not **Off**, so nothing falls through.
- **Admins hear nothing about customer aircraft** unless they pick one of those settings
  themselves. The exception: an organization with no technicians signed in sends these to its
  admins, so somebody always hears, and their setting shows **All customer aircraft** until they
  change it.
- **The aircraft's owners are reminded too**: 30 days and 7 days before an inspection is due,
  when it is due, and when it is overdue; for one counted in hours, 10 hours before and when it
  is due. One message lists everything that crossed a step at once, the most urgent first, and
  an inspection counted both ways ("100 hours or 12 months") gives both figures, whichever comes
  first. An owner who has not signed up is emailed at the address on the aircraft. Calendar
  steps go out in the working day where the organization is.
- **Owners are not reminded about what they already know.** Nothing goes out while the
  inspection is on a job the owner has been told about (booked in or further), nor for a week
  after that job is closed out as completed, while the sign-off is entered. On a job that is only
  requested, the reminder still goes, and names the job instead of asking them to call. When the
  aircraft is at the shop on another job, the reminder says so and asks them to have you add the
  inspection to it. A cancelled job hands the inspection back at once.
- **Nothing goes out about a job held from the owner.** While an inspection is on a job whose
  owner emails are on hold, its reminder waits, still owed, and follows the rules above once you
  lift the hold.
- **Somebody who owns a customer aircraft and works here** gets the due and overdue notices once,
  from the shop side by their own setting, and the earlier owner reminders (30 days, 7 days, 10
  hours) as an owner.

How each person hears (email, push) still follows their **Maintenance reminders** switch.

This is a personal setting. Turning it off for yourself does not stop anybody else being
notified.

## Squawk alerts cannot be turned off

Every squawk filed sends an email and a push to every admin and technician in the organization,
except the person who filed it. There is no switch for it on either surface.

<Callout type="note">
On a busy fleet this is the noisiest thing in the module. If it is too much, the practical
lever is the role: an admin who does not want squawk email and does not need admin rights
can hold a narrower role instead.
</Callout>

## When a reminder is actually sent

Each inspection sends up to two: a warning when it enters its **Due soon** band, and one
when it comes due.

The **Due soon** band is exactly the warning lead you set on the inspection. "Warn me 10
hours out" means the warning goes at 10 hours remaining, not at some separate idea of soon.

An inspection with no warning lead at all sends no warning, only the due notification. The
Add inspections form forces a lead of at least 1 hour or 1 day, so this only shows up on
inspections created another way.

## Hour-based inspections only notify after a close-out

Calendar inspections are checked automatically through the day, so a 12-month annual will
warn you on its own.

Hour-based inspections work differently. Their countdown, their notification and their
automatic grounding all happen at one moment: when somebody closes out a flight on that
aircraft and enters the meters.

<Callout type="warning">
That means an aircraft flown on somebody else's paperwork, or a tach corrected by hand under
<Where app="web">Aircraft → the tail → Edit</Where>, can sit at Overdue on screen with nobody
emailed and the aircraft still bookable. A single closed-out flight on that tail brings
everything back into line. See
[Troubleshooting inspections and squawks](/docs/maintenance/troubleshooting-inspections-and-squawks).
</Callout>

## Where the email link takes you

A maintenance reminder links straight to the inspection it is about, which opens properly in
the iOS app. An owner's reminder opens their aircraft, or their **Home** when it lists more than
one aircraft.

A squawk alert's button lands on that squawk's own page, on both surfaces. Searching its
title from anywhere in the console opens the same page.
