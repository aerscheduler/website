<Summary>
Three separate things live under Maintenance. An **inspection** is what an aircraft owes
on a schedule. A **squawk** is a problem somebody found. **Grounding** is the only one of
the three that actually stops a booking.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins, Dispatchers, Technicians</Roles>

<Where app="web">Maintenance</Where> opens for owners, admins, dispatchers and
technicians. Instructors, students and renters cannot open it at all, on either surface.

## Inspections, squawks, and grounding are three different things

| Thing | What it is | Does it stop a booking? |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection | Something the aircraft owes on a clock: an annual, a 100-hour, a transponder check, an oil change. AerScheduler counts down to it. | Only if that inspection carries the **Grounds the aircraft** flag, and only once it comes due. |
| Squawk | A discrepancy somebody found: "Left brake soft". Filed against one tail, closed out by a technician or an admin. | No, unless whoever filed it also switched on **Grounds the aircraft**. |
| Grounded | A flag and a reason on the aircraft itself. | Yes. Every booking type except maintenance is refused with "Resource is grounded." |

That last row is the one worth remembering. An aircraft with six open squawks and an
overdue annual is still bookable unless somebody or something grounded it.

## An inspection rule is not an inspection

The **rule** lives once for the whole school. "100-hour, every 100 tach hours, warn 10
hours out" is one rule, and you will find it under <Where app="web">Maintenance → Set
up</Where>.

Attaching that rule to a tail creates one live **inspection** per aircraft. That is what
counts down, what shows up in the work queue, and what you sign off.

<Callout type="warning">
Deleting a rule stops tracking on every aircraft it covers, not just the one you were
looking at. And a rule attached to no aircraft is completely inert: it looks configured,
and it never comes due. See
[Change which aircraft an inspection covers](/docs/maintenance/change-or-stop-tracking-an-inspection).
</Callout>

## Hours, days, or a single date

Every inspection is counted one of three ways, chosen when you create it and never
converted into another.

- **On the meter.** Counts flying hours against the aircraft's tach or Hobbs. A 100-hour.
- **On the calendar.** Recurs every so many days from the last sign-off. An annual, a transponder check.
- **One-off.** A single date that never comes back. "Prop back from the shop."

An hour-based inspection never shows a due **date**, and a calendar-based one never shows
a meter reading. That is deliberate. A 100-hour depends entirely on how much the aircraft
flies, so AerScheduler will not invent a date by averaging.

## Tach or Hobbs, and why that is not your billing meter

Each hour-based inspection is set to count one meter, either **Count tach time** or
**Count hobbs time**. Both meters are recorded at every close-out.

The meter an aircraft is **billed on** is a separate setting on the aircraft itself. Most
schools bill on Hobbs and inspect on tach, which is why an inspection's detail line always
names the meter it means: "Due at 1250.0 tach, now 1237.6."

## Due soon means the warning lead you set

Each inspection carries a warning lead: "Warn me 10 hours out", "Warns 30 days out". That
number does two jobs. It is when the row turns to **Due soon**, and it is when the warning
email and push go out. There is no separate cosmetic idea of "soon".

## The Maintenance page, view by view

The left rail holds five views in two groups.

| View | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **By aircraft** | One card per tail, grounded first, then worst-due first. Shows the current tach and the single most urgent item. The default view. |
| **All inspections** | The work queue for the whole school, worst first. Signed-off work is deliberately not here. |
| **Set up** | The rules rather than their instances, grouped into On the meter, On the calendar and One-off. |
| **Squawks → Open** | Every outstanding discrepancy, newest first. |
| **Squawks → Resolved** | The ones already closed out. |

A search box and filter chips sit above the content. On **All inspections** you also get a
Status filter with Overdue, Due soon and Not yet due.

Each aircraft carries the same information for its own tail under
<Where app="web">Aircraft → the tail → Maintenance</Where>: an Inspections card and an
Open squawks card, with Add, Sign off, Log and Resolve on them.

## What the iOS app does that the console does not

The app has the same four boards (Aircraft, Inspections, Squawks, Set up) plus two things
the console cannot do:

- **Report a squawk as any member.** Instructors, students and renters have no way to file
  one from the console, because the Maintenance section of an aircraft's page is hidden
  from them.
- A **Signed off** filter on the Inspections board, behind the filter glyph in the header.
  The console's All inspections view never shows completed work.
