## Resolve it

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open the Open board">
    <Where app="web">Maintenance → Squawks → Open</Where>, newest first. Use the Aircraft
    filter or the search box to narrow it to one tail.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Click Resolve">
    Either on the card itself, or open the card and use **Resolve squawk** in the footer of
    the detail panel.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Set Completed">
    Defaults to today. Change it to the day the work was actually finished. It is required.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Write what was done">
    Part number, work order, what was found. Keep it under 500 characters.

    <Screenshot id="resolve-squawk-modal" />
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="Click Resolve squawk">
    It moves to the Resolved tab with a Resolved badge and a timestamp.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What resolving changes

- The squawk leaves the Open board and appears under **Resolved**, sorted by the date it was resolved.
- It stops appearing in the open-squawk warning on the booking form for that tail.
- A "Squawk resolved" line goes into the audit log, with your notes.
- It is counted as closed in the **Squawks &amp; downtime** report, where "days open" runs from the reported date to the resolved date.

## Verify is a second sign-off

Two stamps, two different acts. **Resolving** records what was done. **Verifying** records
that somebody reproduced or checked the fault. They are independent: a squawk can be
resolved having never been verified, and nothing warns you about it.

Verify has no fields to fill in. It is offered wherever a squawk is, to the same people who
can resolve one:

- <Where app="web">Maintenance → Squawks → Open</Where>, in the footer of the squawk panel.
- <Where app="web">Aircraft → the tail → Maintenance</Where>, on the Open squawks card.
- The squawk's own page, at the top beside **Resolve**.
- In the app, on the squawk from the Squawks board.

<Callout type="warning">
Verify before you resolve. The **Verify** button disappears once a squawk carries either
stamp, on both surfaces, so a squawk that was resolved first can never be verified. If your
shop uses verify as its second signature, make that the habit: verify, then resolve.
</Callout>

## Keep the notes under 500 characters

The notes box counts up to 600 and the save fails above 500, on both surfaces, with
"Couldn't resolve that squawk." If a long write-up will not save, that is why. Shorten it,
or put the detail in the work order and reference the number.

## Dispatchers cannot resolve a squawk

A dispatcher can open the Maintenance page and read every board, including this one, but
there is no **Resolve** button anywhere for them, and no **Verify** either. Reading the
fleet's discrepancies and declaring one fixed are treated as different jobs.

If a dispatcher has been asked to "close out that squawk", they need an admin or a
technician. See [Who can do what in maintenance](/docs/maintenance/who-can-do-what-in-maintenance).

## Resolving does not return a grounded aircraft to service

<Callout type="important">
If the aircraft is grounded, resolving the squawk leaves it grounded. That is true whether
the grounding came from the squawk, from an inspection with a hand-typed reason, or from an
admin grounding it directly. An admin has to open the aircraft and click **Return to
service**. The only grounding AerScheduler lifts by itself is one it created, from an
inspection, and it lifts that when the inspection is signed off.
</Callout>
