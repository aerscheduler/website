## Report one from the iOS app

Anybody can file a squawk from the app, including instructors, students and renters. Open
the aircraft, then **Report Squawk** from the gear menu.

## Report one from a booking

Open the booking on the schedule. **Report a squawk** sits in its close-out, under whatever
the booking is waiting on, with the tail already filled in.

This is the route for an instructor, a student or a renter working in the console. The
Maintenance page and the aircraft page's Maintenance section are staff-only, so the booking
you just flew is where the action lives for everybody else. It shows on any booking with an
aircraft on it, from the moment it is on the board until it is billed.

Ground lessons and simulator bookings do not offer it, because there is no airframe to squawk.

## The app asks after every flight

Close a flight out on the phone and, right after you enter the Hobbs and tach readings, the
**Report Squawk** sheet opens on its own with the aircraft already filled in. Fill it in and
save, or tap **Skip**. Either way the close-out carries on to the completion sheet.

The console does not interrupt you the same way. It keeps **Report a squawk** on the booking
instead, which is one press away for as long as the booking is open.

## Title and description are both required

The **Log squawk** button stays greyed out until both are filled in. It used to grey out on
a missing title alone, so a squawk with no description could be submitted and came back with
a red "Description is required." on a form that had looked complete.

## Keep the title under 50 characters

The console form has no character counter and no length warning. A title over 50 characters,
or a description over 500, fails with a message that names nothing: "Unable to create
squawk." If you get that and everything looks filled in, shorten the title first, then the
description. The app caps both fields correctly as you type.

## Grounding the aircraft from the squawk form

The console's Log a squawk dialog carries a **Grounds the aircraft** switch. Left off, which
is the default, filing a squawk changes nothing about whether the aircraft can be booked. It
is a note for the shop, not a no-go.

Switch it on and the tail is grounded as the squawk is filed, and the squawk itself carries
a Grounding badge.

<Callout type="warning">
Resolving a grounding squawk later does not put the aircraft back on the line. An admin has
to click **Return to service** on the aircraft when the work is done. See
[Ground an aircraft, and return it to service](/docs/maintenance/ground-an-aircraft).
</Callout>

If you did not use the switch and the aircraft needs to come off the line, ground it
separately from its own page. Only an admin or owner can do that.

## Who gets told

Every admin and technician in the school, except the person who filed it, gets an email and
a push notification titled "Squawk reported on" the tail.

Dispatchers are not notified, even though they can read the squawk boards. Nobody can turn
squawk alerts off. See
[Who gets maintenance reminders and squawk alerts](/docs/maintenance/who-gets-maintenance-reminders-and-squawk-alerts).

A "Squawk filed" line is also written to the audit log.
