<Where app="web">Settings → Airworthiness Directives</Where> has one switch, **Find the ADs for
our aircraft**. It is off by default. Turned on, AerScheduler lists the Airworthiness Directives
that name each of your aircraft for somebody to decide, and alerts you when a new one is
published.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins</Roles>

## Why it is off by default

Plenty of organizations use AerScheduler for oil changes, annuals and the 100-hour, and have no
interest in us being involved in Airworthiness Directives. Plenty of others already pay for
ADlog, AVTRAK or a Tdata subscription and have a mechanic who owns the whole question.

Turning it on for them would put several hundred ADs to review in front of people who never
asked, attached to a subject where a flood of alerts teaches people to stop reading alerts. So it
stays off until an owner or admin turns it on.

With it on:

- Each aircraft gets an **ADs** tab listing the Airworthiness Directives that name its make and model, its engine or its propeller. See
  [Review the ADs for an aircraft](/docs/maintenance/review-the-ads-for-an-aircraft).
- **Maintenance → Fleet ADs** lists every aircraft's ADs in one place, and **Reports → AD status**
  is what you hand an inspector. See
  [See every aircraft's ADs](/docs/maintenance/airworthiness-directives-across-the-fleet).
- Owners, admins and technicians are told when a newly published AD names one of your aircraft,
  and get a weekly email of what is still undecided.

With it off, maintenance works exactly as before. You can still set an inspection's **Where this
comes from** to *Airworthiness Directive* for the badge and the permanent compliance record; see
[Track Airworthiness Directives](/docs/maintenance/track-airworthiness-directives).

<Callout type="important">
AerScheduler never decides whether an Airworthiness Directive applies to your aircraft. A listed
AD is one worth reading, not one that applies; an owner, admin or technician decides each one,
and the decision keeps who made it and when. An AD the library
does not hold can still be tracked by hand.
</Callout>

## What makes the list complete

The list matches by the make and model of the airframe, the engine and the propeller, so each one
you record finds more:

- **Engine.** Filled in from the FAA registry for US aircraft, and on the aircraft form when you
  look up a tail. Check it on the aircraft page if engine ADs are missing.
- **Propeller.** The FAA registry does not record propellers, so type it on the aircraft form.
- **Serial number.** Never used to leave an AD off, because a misread range would hide one that
  applies. It is what the person reviewing checks an AD's serial range against, and the
  **Airworthiness compliance** report prints it beside the tail.

Each aircraft's **ADs** tab says what is missing for that aircraft, in the info button beside its
search.

## Who can change this

Owners and admins. Not an office manager with the settings grant, and not a technician. Turning it
on changes what every technician at the organization is asked to review, which makes it a decision
about how the organization runs rather than a preference.

## Turning it off

Nothing is deleted. Compliance records are permanent regardless, and the decisions made on each
aircraft's **ADs** tab are kept, so an organization that turns it off and on again finds them
where it left them. While it is off the AD lists, the alerts and the weekly email stop.
