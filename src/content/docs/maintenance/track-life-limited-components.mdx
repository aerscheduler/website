<Summary>
Open the aircraft, then <Where app="web">Maintenance → Components → Add</Where>. Enter the part,
when it went on, the aircraft's tach then, its own hours since new or overhaul then, and its limit
in hours, months, or both. The card shows its time now and the life left, and each limit is
tracked as an inspection, so it warns, reminds owners and grounds like the rest.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins, Technicians</Roles>

Dispatchers can read the card. Instructors, students and renters do not see it. It works the
same on your own aircraft and on a customer's aircraft in the shop.

## Add a component

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open the aircraft's Maintenance tab">
    <Where app="web">Aircraft → the tail → Maintenance</Where>. **Components** sits under
    **Inspections**. Click **Add**.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Name it and say where it is">
    **Name** is what you call it ("Left magneto", "Propeller"). **Position** is where it sits:
    pick Engine, Propeller, Airframe or Avionics, or type your own. **Part number** and **Serial
    number** go on the inspection, so whoever signs it off knows which part it is.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Say when it went on, and its time then">
    **Installed on** is the day it went on. **Aircraft tach when installed** is the aircraft's
    reading that day; it starts at today's reading, so change it for a part that went on
    earlier. **Its time when installed** is the part's own hours then. Use the **Since new**
    chip to switch to **Since overhaul** for an overhauled part.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Give the limit">
    **Life limit, hours** counts its own time. **Life limit, months** counts the calendar. Give
    one or both: with both, whichever comes first. Months count from the day it was new or last
    overhauled, so set that with the **Overhaul date** chip when it is not the day it went on.
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="Click Save">
    The part appears with its time now and the life it has left, and an inspection named
    **Life limit:** and the part's name appears under **Inspections**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the card

Each row shows the part, where it is, its part and serial numbers, and on the right the life
left on whichever limit is nearer: "40.0 h left", "60 days left", or "Until Mar 14, 2027" when it
is further out. Underneath is its time now ("460.0 h since overhaul of 500.0, on the tach") and
the calendar limit's date.

- **Amber** inside 10% of a limit or 10 hours, whichever is more. On a calendar limit, inside
  10% of its life or 30 days.
- **Past limit** once it is reached, with how far past.
- **Inspection** on the right opens the inspection that tracks its limit.

Its time is worked out from the aircraft's tach: the hours it had when it went on, plus every hour
the tach has moved since. Keep the aircraft's times current and the card stays current. Choose
the **On the Hobbs** chip for a part whose limit counts Hobbs time instead.

## It is tracked like any inspection

Each limit is kept as a one-off inspection on the aircraft, due at the tach reading (or on the day)
the part reaches it. That is what makes it work with everything else:

- the shop is warned as it gets close, at the steps set on the inspection, and on a customer's
  aircraft the owners are reminded too;
- it shows in the inspection lists with the rest, and on a customer's aircraft in the owner's
  **Coming due**;
- when it is reached, the aircraft is grounded, unless you chose **Does not ground** with the
  **Grounds at the limit** chip.

Change the part, not the inspection. Editing the component's times or limit moves its inspection's
due point; deleting that inspection on its own is refused, because the part would then claim a
limit nothing tracks.

<Callout type="warning">
When the part is replaced or overhauled, **record it**: use **Record it came off** in the row's
**…** menu, then add the new one (or edit the time and the overhaul date for a part overhauled and
refitted). Signing off its inspection on its own clears that inspection, but the card still counts
the old part's time.
</Callout>

## A part comes off

**…** on the row, then **Record it came off**. Give the day and the aircraft's tach then. Its time
stops there, its inspection is removed (one already signed off stays in the history), and it moves
under **Show components that came off**. **Put back on** in the same menu brings it back, with its
tracking.

**Delete** is only for a part entered by mistake. It takes the record with it.

## On a customer's aircraft

The aircraft's owners see the parts and the life they have left on their aircraft's page, without
your notes. See [See your aircraft and approve work](/docs/maintenance/see-your-aircraft-and-approve-work).

## What it does not do

- It does not know any manufacturer's limits. Enter the limit from the part's documentation.
- An aircraft with no meters, a glider for instance, can only carry a limit in months.
- A replaced tach is not caught: if the aircraft's tach was replaced since the part went on, edit
  the part's time and the tach reading it went on at.
