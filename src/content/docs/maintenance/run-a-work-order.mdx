<Summary>
<Where app="web">Maintenance → Open jobs → Open a work order</Where>. Pick the aircraft, check
who pays, write down what the owner asked for, and click **Open work order**. The job gets the
next number (WO-1001, WO-1002 and so on) and its own page, where you move it through its stages
until the aircraft goes home.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins, Technicians</Roles>

A work order is one job on one aircraft: who is billed, when it came in and when you promised
it back, what the owner asked for, and where the job stands. Only owners, admins and technicians
see work orders. Dispatchers see customer aircraft on the schedule and the hangar bookings, but
not the jobs, and pilots never do, not even on your own aircraft, because a job carries the
owner's details and prices.

## Open a work order

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Start from the board or the aircraft">
    <Where app="web">Maintenance → Open jobs → Open a work order</Where>, the **+** in the top bar
    from any page, or open the aircraft and go to <Where app="web">Work orders → Open a work
    order</Where>. From the aircraft, the tail is already chosen.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Pick the aircraft, or add it">
    Search the tail. A customer's aircraft that is not on file yet is added from here: type the
    tail and choose **Add a customer aircraft** at the bottom of the list. The tail you typed is
    filled in, and once you save it the job picks it. Only admins add aircraft.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Check who pays">
    **Billed to** fills itself in with the aircraft's owner marked **Billed** on its **Owners**
    panel. One person pays for each job: co-owners are not billed. Choose **Nobody** for your
    own aircraft. You can change it later, until the job is invoiced. Only an admin chooses who
    pays; a job a technician opens goes to the aircraft's owner.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Pick the stage">
    **Requested** when the owner has asked for the work but no day is agreed, **Scheduled** when a
    day is agreed for it to come in, **Aircraft received** when it is in the hangar. Each stage
    says what it means in the menu. Before the aircraft arrives there are no meters to record, so
    the form does not ask. Choosing **Aircraft received** fills in the Hobbs and tach in from the
    aircraft; correct them if the meters say something different.
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="Write down what they asked for">
    In the owner's words: "Annual inspection. Left brake feels soft." Add **Promised back** if you
    gave them a day, and assign the technicians doing the work. Only people with the technician
    role are listed: give an admin who does the work that role too.
  </Step>
  <Step n={6} title="Click Open work order">
    You land on the job's own page.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Move the job along

On the job's page, the **Stage** menu at the top moves it: Requested, Scheduled, Aircraft
received, In progress, Waiting for owner, Waiting for parts, Ready for pickup, Completed, or
Cancelled. The dates follow the stage: the first stage that means the aircraft is here records
when it was **received**, **Completed** records when the work finished, and **Completed** or
**Cancelled** moves the job to **Finished jobs** with that day. Moving a finished job back to an
open stage puts it back on the board. Moving a job back to **Requested** or **Scheduled** clears
the received date and the Hobbs and tach in and out, so the day the aircraft really arrives, and
its meters then, are the ones recorded.

When a job moves to **Aircraft received** (or straight to a later stage in the hangar) with no
Hobbs or tach in recorded, the meters open under **Details** with them filled in from the
aircraft. Check them against the meters and click **Save**, or **Cancel** to leave them empty.

Moving a job to **Ready for pickup** or **Completed** while an inspection on it is not signed off
(and the owner did not decline or put it off) asks first. Sign it off, or choose **Complete
anyway**: while the job is open the owner hears nothing about that inspection, and once it is
finished they start getting its reminders.

<Callout type="note">
A reading lower than the one the aircraft last had, or a Hobbs or tach out lower than in, asks
you to check it before it saves. Save it anyway only if that is what the meters say, for example
after a meter was replaced.
</Callout>

There is no Edit button: each detail is changed where it is shown. Click a value under
**Details** to change it: **Billed to**, **Promised back**, **Technicians**, the **Hobbs** and
**tach** in and out, and **Booked for**, the maintenance booking on the schedule that holds the
aircraft and the hangar for the job (choose **Not booked** when it has none). It works from the
other side too: a maintenance booking has a **Work order** field that links an open job or
opens a new one with its technicians. Click the
pencil on a notes card to write in it, then **Save** (or Cmd+Enter); Esc puts it back. **What the
owner asked for** is the request in their words, **Notes for the owner** are what you would tell
them, and **Shop notes** are for the shop only.

<Callout type="note">
The Hobbs and tach on a work order are what the job recorded. They never change the aircraft's
own meters, so typing a reading on a job cannot ground the aircraft or move an inspection.
</Callout>

## The work on the job

The job's page keeps the work and what it charges in one list, **Work and charges**. What the
owner asked for and what you found are separate groups, because they are different conversations
with the owner, and each item has the labor and parts done for it listed underneath, with its
total. Click **Add** and choose **Owner's request** or **Finding**, or type to search the menu.
Hover a group's header and click its **+** to add straight into that group: an owner's request,
a finding, or a charge not for any item.

- **Each item's ring says where it stands:** a green tick when it is done, a blue dot when the
  owner approved it, a dashed ring when the owner has not answered yet, a half-filled ring while
  its inspection or squawk is open.
- **Fold** a group or an item by clicking it. With the keyboard, the arrow keys move down the
  list, Enter opens a line, and Left and Right fold an item.

- **Attach an inspection or a squawk** that is open on this aircraft, and the item is done when it
  is signed off or resolved, whether that happens here or on the phone. Its **Sign off** or
  **Resolve** button opens the same form as everywhere else, so the next due date is worked out
  as it always is.
- **A plain item** (a brake adjustment, a wash) you tick done yourself.
- A found item you have not sent to the owner says **Not sent yet**; once sent it says **Sent,
  waiting** until they answer. Click that tag to record an answer you took on the phone straight
  from the list: **Approved**, **Declined** or **Deferred**, or **Record a call with notes…** for
  the full record below. A finding done before anybody answered says **Done, not asked**, or
  **Done, no answer** if it had been sent.
- **Rewording a finding the owner was asked about asks again.** Their answer was for the old
  words, so it is cleared and the finding reads **Not sent yet**: send it again. A finding not
  sent yet, an owner's request, or a finding already done rewords freely. On a job that is
  invoiced or closed nothing can be asked again, so rewording only corrects the words and the
  owner's answer stands, as the bill does.
- An item's **⋯** menu adds labor or a part for it, marks it done, renames it or removes it. A
  finding you never sent that still has charges on it cannot be removed: its lines would stay on
  the bill for something the owner never heard of. Take its lines off first, or send it.

## Send findings to the owner

When you have written up what you found, and priced it if you can, click **Send to owner** at the
top of **Work and charges**. Every owner of the aircraft is told in one message and asked to
approve, decline or put off each finding. Prices and the total go only to the person the job is
billed to.

- **Until you send a finding, the owners do not see it.** Write it up and price it first; send
  when it is ready. Adding a finding to an open job that is not invoiced also offers **Notify
  owner** in the message that confirms it, for when it is ready as it stands; it sends every
  finding not sent yet.
- **Only what is new is sent.** Findings already sent, answered or done are left out, so pressing
  it again later sends just the ones added since.
- **A job opened while you set up your shop starts with owner emails held.** The line at the top
  of the job says so; **Tell the owner** sends the owner where the job stands (booked in, arrived
  or ready), and sending findings releases the hold too. From then on the owners hear each stage
  as usual.
- **The job moves to Waiting for owner** if it was at Aircraft received or In progress, and back
  to In progress by itself once every finding sent has an answer or is done: the owner answers in
  AerScheduler, you record their call, you tick it done, or you sign off its inspection or
  resolve its squawk (here or on the phone). Only answering or finishing a finding that was
  waiting moves it back: a job you set to **Waiting for owner** yourself, for something else,
  stays there. Unticking a finding sent and not answered moves the job back to Waiting for owner.
- **Only on a customer's aircraft.** On your organization's own aircraft there is no owner to
  send to or call. The tag on a finding reads **Not decided**, and its menu, **Decision**, records
  your choice under your name. **Record owner's answer** and the **Owner's answers** card are not
  shown.
- **An owner who has not signed up** is emailed at the address on the aircraft, with a way to
  sign up and answer. One with no address is listed as not reached, and if nobody can be reached
  nothing is marked sent.

## Record the owner's answer

When you have spoken to the owner, click **Record owner's answer**, beside the count of findings
waiting at the top of **Work and charges**. Put who you spoke to, when,
the most they said you can spend without calling again, and notes. Then choose **Approve**,
**Decline** or **Defer** for each item you discussed. Only the items you answer are changed, and
an item answered again on a later call shows the later answer.

Leave **When** as it is and the call is recorded at the moment you save it. Change it to write up
an earlier call: if the owner answered an item later than that (on another call, or themselves in
AerScheduler), their later answer stands, and the message after saving says which items kept it.

Once the job is invoiced, its answers are the bill's too. Each finding's tag shows its answer but
no longer opens, **Record owner's answer** is gone, and the top of **Work and charges** says
**Invoiced: void the invoice to change the work.** The same holds on a closed job: reopen it
first.

<Callout type="note">
An owner who has signed in can answer what you sent them themselves, until the job is invoiced,
and the technicians on the job are told when they do. When they call instead, this is
your record of the call: who you spoke to, when, what they agreed to, and who took the call. Every
answer is listed under **Owner's answers** on the job.
</Callout>

## Photos and documents

**Files and photos** on the job keeps the pictures and the paperwork: the crack you found, the
logbook entry, the 8130-3 for a part. Click **Attach** and choose up to five photos or PDFs at
a time. To put a photo on one finding, use that item's menu, **Attach a photo or file**; the
item's row shows how many it has.

Every file is the shop's own until you choose **Show to the owner** from its menu (and **Keep
to the shop** puts it back). Its menu also moves it onto a different item or the job itself,
and removes it. Files can still be added after the job is invoiced, since the logbook entry
is often the last thing that happens.

## Labor and parts

The lines are everything the job will charge. Anyone in the shop adds lines as the work is done:
a technician enters their own hours and the parts they fitted. Click **Add** and choose the kind
under **Charges**, or use an item's menu, **Add labor or a part for it**, to add a line already
under that item. Click a line to change it.

The form asks only for what the line is: what was done, and its hours and rate or its quantity
and price. Everything else is a row of buttons under it, each showing its value, and a line that
keeps the usual values never needs them: the **kind**, the **item** it is for, the
**technician** and **day** (labor), the part's **status** and **expected** date, a **discount**,
**sales tax**, and whether it is **billed**. Click one to change it.

<Callout type="note">
Only an admin prices. Technicians enter work at the shop's rates: their labor
takes the shop's labor rate and their parts and outside work the shop's markup. They type a
price only on Shop supplies, Freight, Fee and Other lines. An admin changes a line's rate,
markup, price or kind, decides its tax and whether it is billed, and changes lines somebody
else entered. Everyone else changes only the lines they entered, and a line an admin prices
becomes the admin's. An admin sees every line in the invoice preview before raising it.
</Callout>

- **Labor** is hours at a rate. Type clock time: 1.5 is an hour and a half, 0.25 a quarter.
  **Rate per hour** fills in from the shop's labor rate; an admin can change it on the entry.
  Until the shop has a labor rate, only an admin can enter labor.
  Pick the **Technician** and the **Day** the work was done.
- **Part** and **Outside work** are what the shop paid (**Cost each**) plus the shop's markup for
  that kind. **Price each** shows the result, greyed, and says the markup under it. An admin can
  type a price there to charge that instead; empty the field to go back to cost plus the markup.
  A part also takes its part number, serial number and vendor, and a status (ordered, received,
  installed, returned, unused) with the day it was ordered and the day it is expected.
- **Shop supplies**, **Freight and shipping**, **Fee** and **Other** are a quantity at a price.
- **Discount** takes a percentage off a whole line: 10 takes 10% off. Only an admin gives one. The
  card and the invoice show the line at its discounted price, with "(10% off)" in its name, and a
  line 100% off stays on the invoice at $0.00 so the owner sees the work you gave them.

Each line can point at one of the job's items with **For item**, and the list shows it under that
item, so you can check the charges against the work the owner approved. A line for no item, or
for an item since removed, is listed under **Not for a specific item**.

- **Billed** is the default. Choose **No charge** for warranty work or shop time you are not
  billing: the line stays on the job as a record of the work, and never reaches the invoice.
- **Sales tax** follows your organization's rule for that kind of line. Choose **Taxable** or **Not
  taxable** on a line to decide it yourself. See
  [Charge sales tax](/docs/billing/charge-sales-tax) for the rules.

<Callout type="note">
The customer never sees your cost or your markup. The invoice shows the price each and the
total, with the part number and "(4 x $12.50)" in the line's name when there is more than one.
All of a job's labor lines stay separate, each saying its hours, and its rate when the line is
charged at that rate.
</Callout>

## Raise the invoice

<Roles>Owners, Admins</Roles>

When the work is done, click **Raise invoice** at the bottom of **Work and charges**, beside the
total. The dialog prices
the bill exactly as it will be raised: each line with its tax, your service fee, and the total.
Under **Also on the invoice** it shows what the bill carries besides the lines: the work order
number, the aircraft (tail, make and model), the Hobbs and tach in and out, and a description
listing the work completed (the items marked done that the owner did not decline) followed by
your **Notes for the owner**. Your notes always keep their room: a long list of finished work ends
"and 3 more", and the dialog says when anything was shortened. Pick when it is **Due**: on
receipt, in 15 or 30 days, or **On a date…** to pick the day on a calendar (up to a year out; it
is due at the end of that day). Then click the button that names the total. The person billed
gets it by email with a link to pay online: an owner who is not a member
of your organization gets Stripe's email
at the address on the aircraft's **Owners** panel, and a member gets it by email and in the app.
Or you record cash or a check the usual way.

- **Billed to** must name somebody. A job billed to **Nobody** cannot be invoiced: choose who
  pays under **Details**.
- An owner who is not a member is reached only at the email on the aircraft's **Owners** panel,
  until they sign in: then the invoice reaches them at their own login, like a member. With
  neither, the invoice is refused until you add an address.
- **Findings the owner never heard about are named first.** A finding with charges that was never
  sent to the owner, answered or done is listed in an amber box, and the invoice is not raised
  until you tick **Bill them anyway**. Send it first, record the owner's answer, mark it done, or
  take its lines off if the owner should not pay for it yet.
- **So are findings still waiting on the owner's answer.** Once the job is invoiced the owner can
  no longer answer them, so they get an amber box of their own and the same tick. Record the
  owner's answer if they told you on the phone, mark them done, or take their lines off.
- Findings the owner declined or put off that still have charges are listed too, as a reminder.
- If a line or a rate changed while the dialog was open, the invoice is not raised. The dialog
  shows the new total to check, and you raise it again. The same goes for the notes for the owner
  and the finished work: the owner is never sent text you did not see. The same happens if somebody added a line
  in the moment the invoice was being made: that invoice is voided at once, so the bill always
  matches the job.
- An invoice can be at most $999,999.99, and so can a line.

Once the invoice is raised, the job's lines and the owner's answers are frozen, the job cannot be
cancelled, and who pays cannot change. The owner's page shows the bill from then on: every line
on it, and nothing left for them to answer. To correct anything, void the invoice in
<Where app="web">Billing</Where>, change the lines, and raise a new one. The job's **Billing**
card links to its invoice, and the invoice links back to the job.

<Callout type="warning">
Voiding the invoice does not let you delete the job. A bill happened, so the job stays on the
record: set it to **Cancelled** if the work is not going ahead.
</Callout>

## Shop rates

<Roles>Owners, Admins</Roles>

<Where app="web">Settings → Shop rates</Where> sets the **Labor rate per hour**, the **Markup on
parts**, and the **Markup on outside work** (a propeller overhaul, a radio repair sent out).
Markups are percentages: 15 adds 15% to what the shop paid.

These price NEW lines only. Each line keeps the rate and markup it was entered with, so changing
the shop's rates never changes a job already in progress. Only owners and admins see this page; a
technician sees the rate filled in on each labor line, and only an admin changes it there.

## Find a job

- <Where app="web">Maintenance → Open jobs</Where> groups the open jobs by where the aircraft is:
  **In the hangar**, **Waiting** (on the owner or a part), **Ready for pickup**, **Scheduled** and
  **Requested**, oldest first in each, with who pays, the technicians, the promised day (amber
  once it has passed) and what the job has charged so far. Click a column's name to sort by it,
  inside each group; click again to reverse it, and a third time to go back. Click a job to see it
  beside the list, or double-click it to open its page. **Finished jobs** lists completed and
  cancelled ones, newest first.
- The filter button beside the search narrows either list by **Assigned: To me**,
  **Aircraft**, **Location** (where the aircraft is based), **Owner** (anybody on the
  aircraft's Owners panel, billed or not), **Billed to**, and **Technician**. Each takes
  several at once. A technician's boards open on their own jobs; clear the filter to see
  everyone's.
- On an aircraft, <Where app="web">Work orders</Where> shows its open jobs and its past ones.
- On a person in <Where app="web">People</Where>, **Work orders** lists the jobs billed to
  them, open first. It is always there for an aircraft owner who is not a member, and on a
  member once they have been billed for a job. Past the five most recent finished jobs, a link
  opens the rest on **Finished jobs**.
- Search with ⌘K for the job number (1042 or WO-1042), the tail, or the owner's name. Type
  `jobs:` first to search work orders only.

On the Aircraft page's **Customer aircraft** tab, each aircraft says **In the shop** while it has
an open job and is here, **Expected in** when its job is requested or scheduled, and **Not in
the shop** otherwise.

## Delete a job opened by mistake

An admin can delete a job from the menu on its page, as long as it has never been invoiced. A
job that has been invoiced stays on the record: set its stage to **Cancelled** instead. The
notifications about a deleted job go with it, the shop's and the owners'.
Deleting leaves a line in the audit log saying the job existed and who deleted it.

## Every change is recorded

Opening a job, changing its stage, editing it, adding or changing a line, recording the owner's
answer, and raising the invoice each leave a line in the audit log, naming the job, the aircraft
and the person billed. Admins can find them under <Where app="web">Audit log → Work orders</Where>.
Changing the shop's rates is recorded too, under <Where app="web">Audit log → Settings</Where>.
