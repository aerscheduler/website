<Summary>
Click **Sign off** on the inspection row, set **Completed on** to the day the work actually
finished, correct the meter reading, add your notes, then click **Sign off**. A repeating
inspection starts counting again immediately, from exactly those two numbers.
</Summary>

## Enter the meter reading from the day the work was done, not today

This is the field that matters most, and it is the one that looks like a display value.

The **next** interval counts from the number you type. Sign off a 100-hour with today's
tach when the work was actually done six hours ago and the next 100-hour comes due six
hours late. Permanently, on every cycle after that, with nothing on screen to say so.

<Callout type="warning">
Same rule in the other direction: if the shop finished the annual on the 3rd and you sign
it off on the 12th, put the 3rd in **Completed on**. The next annual is measured from what
you type, not from when you typed it.
</Callout>

## What happens next

- The item leaves **All inspections**. That view is a work queue and only holds outstanding work.
- If the rule repeats, a fresh inspection is created straight away, counting from your completed date and completed meter reading.
- A one-off does not come back. Once it is signed off on every tail it covers, the rule disappears from the Set up list entirely.
- An "Inspection signed off" line is written to the audit log with your name on it.

## When the aircraft comes back on the line automatically

If the aircraft was grounded automatically by an inspection coming due, its grounding reason
is the single word "Maintenance". Sign off the last outstanding item holding it and
AerScheduler returns the aircraft to service by itself. The dialog says as much before you
confirm.

Every overdue inspection on that tail has to be signed off first, not only the ones that
ground. An overdue VOR check or oil change is enough to keep the aircraft grounded.

## When it does not

<Callout type="warning">
If somebody grounded the aircraft **by hand** and typed their own reason ("Prop strike",
"Annual in progress"), signing inspections off will never lift it. Only the automatic
reason "Maintenance" is ever cleared automatically, because AerScheduler will not overrule
a human decision. An admin has to click **Return to service** on the aircraft. See
[Ground an aircraft, and return it to service](/docs/maintenance/ground-an-aircraft).
</Callout>

## Notes, and the 500 character limit

The console stops you at 500 characters. The app's sign-off sheet lets you keep typing past
that, and then the save fails with "Couldn't sign that off." If you get that message and
the aircraft and dates look fine, shorten the notes.

## Where signed-off work goes

It stays on the aircraft's record and in the audit log. It just leaves the queue.

The console's **All inspections** view can never show completed work, and the Status filter
there (Overdue, Due soon, Not yet due) has no "Signed off" option on purpose. The app's
Inspections board does have one, behind the filter glyph, so reach for the phone when
somebody asks you to prove a 100-hour was signed off.

<Callout type="tip">
The **Maintenance due** report under <Where app="web">Reports</Where> carries resolved items
along with outstanding ones, with who signed them off and their notes. That is the export
to hand somebody who wants the paperwork.
</Callout>
