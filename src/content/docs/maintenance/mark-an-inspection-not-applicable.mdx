<Summary>
Open the inspection, find **Applies to this aircraft**, click **Mark not applicable** and say
why. That one inspection on that one aircraft stops being reminded about, counted overdue or
grounding the aircraft. The rule and every other aircraft it covers are unchanged. Click
**It applies again** to put it back.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins, Technicians</Roles>

## When to use it

Some inspections a rule puts on every aircraft do not apply to one of them:

- a VOR check on a VFR-only aircraft with no VOR receiver,
- a 100-hour inspection on an aircraft that is never flown for hire,
- a transponder check on an aircraft with no transponder fitted.

Marking it not applicable keeps it on the aircraft's list, with your reason beside it, so
anybody reading the list can see it was considered and why it does not apply. Deleting it
instead would leave no trace, and removing the aircraft from the rule changes the rule.

## Mark it

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open the inspection">
    <Where app="web">Maintenance → Inspections</Where>, then double-click the inspection, or
    open the aircraft and click it on the Inspections card.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Click Mark not applicable on the Applies to this aircraft card">
    The card is in the right-hand column of the inspection's page.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Say why, then click Mark not applicable">
    The reason is optional, and it is shown wherever the inspection is listed, the aircraft
    owner's own view included. "VFR only, no VOR receiver" says enough.
  </Step>
</Steps>

From then on the inspection reads **Not applicable** with your reason in every list, sorts
to the bottom, and has its own group when the list is grouped by status. The card says who
marked it and when.

## What it stops

While an inspection is marked not applicable:

- No warning, due or overdue notice goes to the shop, and no reminder goes to the aircraft's
  owners.
- It never grounds the aircraft, whatever its dates or meter readings say.
- It is not counted as overdue or due soon anywhere: the aircraft's row, the fleet counts,
  the dashboard and the Maintenance due report.

<Callout type="important">
If the aircraft is grounded because this inspection came due, marking it not applicable
returns the aircraft to service, unless something else is still holding it: another overdue
inspection that grounds, or a grounding squawk. That is the same check signing an inspection
off makes.
</Callout>

## It stays marked after a sign-off

If you sign the inspection off anyway, the next cycle of the rule starts on the same aircraft
already marked not applicable, with the same reason. It stays that way until somebody says it
applies again.

## Put it back

Click **It applies again** on the same card. The inspection is checked straight away, the way
a new inspection is:

- If it is already overdue and its rule grounds the aircraft, the aircraft comes off the line
  as soon as you save, and the shop is told it is due.
- Warnings start again from where its clocks stand now.

## Scheduled and In progress

An inspection that is an item on an open work order shows which job has it booked in:

| Tag | Means |
|---|---|
| **Scheduled** | The job is Requested or Scheduled: the aircraft is coming in for it. |
| **In progress** | The job has the aircraft at the shop: received, in progress, waiting for the owner or parts, or ready for pickup. |

The tag names the job, and clicking it opens the job. It is worked out from the job, so
nothing has to be kept in step: when the job is completed or cancelled, or the owner declines
or defers that item, the tag goes away. An inspection can be **Scheduled** and still be
overdue; the tag never hides that.

Dispatchers see the inspection's status but not the job, the same as everywhere else work
orders appear. Aircraft owners see the tag on their own aircraft.
