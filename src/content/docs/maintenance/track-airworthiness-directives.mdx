<Roles>Owners, Admins, Technicians</Roles>

## Why an AD is not just another reminder

An Airworthiness Directive is binding under 14 CFR Part 39. It is federal law, not the
manufacturer's advice, and flying out of compliance breaks 91.403(a). A Service Bulletin,
by contrast, is advisory.

That difference has a practical consequence: **91.417 asks you to keep a record**, per
airframe, of the AD number and revision, what you did to comply, the time in service when
you did it, and when the next action falls due. A reminder that has simply gone away does
not satisfy that.

<Callout type="note">
Recurring ADs are usually written "every 100 hours or 12 months, whichever comes first".
That is the **Whichever comes first** interval, so you do not need anything special to
schedule one. What this page adds is saying which rule it is, and keeping the sign-off.
</Callout>

## Tell an inspection it is an AD

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open Maintenance and click Add inspections">
    Choose **Recurring** for a repeating AD, or **One-off** for one with a single
    compliance deadline.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Set the interval as usual">
    For "every 100 hours or 12 months, whichever comes first", pick **Whichever comes
    first** and fill in both clocks.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Open Where this comes from and choose Airworthiness Directive">
    Three more fields appear. They stay hidden for an ordinary shop reminder, so an oil
    change does not grow boxes it has no answers for.

    <Screenshot id="add-inspections-source" />
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Enter the document number">
    The AD number as published, for example `2015-19-07`. This one is required: an AD
    without its number cannot be found, filtered, or listed on the report an inspector
    reads.
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="Add the revision and link if you have them">
    The revision currently in force, and a link to the issuing authority's copy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An **AD** badge now appears on that inspection everywhere it is listed, so a regulation is
distinguishable from a shop interval at a glance.

## When the AD is superseded

ADs get revised, and the revision you are tracking has to be the one currently in force.
Open **Maintenance → Set up**, find the inspection, and click the pencil. Change the
revision, correct a mistyped document number, rename it, or add the link.

<Callout type="important">
Editing here changes the rule **going forward only**. Every compliance record already
signed keeps the number and revision it was signed under, because a record is meant to say
what the mechanic actually complied with on the day. Move an inspection from Rev 2 to Rev 3
and last year's sign-off still reads Rev 2, which is the truth.
</Callout>

An inspection cannot be left as an Airworthiness Directive with no number, whether you are
creating it or editing it. Clear the number and the console asks for one back.

## Sign it off, and keep the record

When you sign an AD off, the sign-off form opens with **Keep a compliance record** already
switched on.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Fill in what was done">
    The method of compliance. This is the sentence an inspector actually reads, so write it
    the way you would write it in the logbook: "Visual and dye-penetrant inspection of the
    forward spar carry-through per paragraph (g)(1). No cracking found."
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Check both meters">
    Tach and Hobbs are both captured, whatever the inspection was counting, because 91.417
    asks for time in service and not for whichever clock the schedule happened to use. They
    are filled in from the aircraft, so usually you are confirming rather than typing.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Confirm who certified it">
    Your name and certificate fill in from your profile. Change them if somebody else
    signed: an outside IA has no account here, and the record has to say who actually
    certified the work.

    <Screenshot id="sign-off-compliance" />
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
A compliance record cannot be edited or deleted once it is signed, by anyone, including us.
That is what makes it a record. If something is wrong, sign off a correcting entry: the log
keeps both, in order.
</Callout>

Signing off is a single action, kept whole. An inspection already signed off answers "That
inspection has already been signed off" rather than recording the work twice, so a
double-tap on a phone, a retried request on a bad connection, or two people in the hangar
signing the same inspection at the same moment all leave exactly one record. And if
anything at all fails partway, nothing is written: you never end up with a permanent record
attached to an inspection still showing as due.

## Set your certificate once

<Where app="web">Profile → Mechanic certificate</Where> holds your FAA certificate number
and type. Enter it once and it fills in every time you sign something off.

This is worth two minutes. Six to eight exact characters, typed at every signature, is how
a compliance log ends up full of blanks and typos, and a record with the wrong certificate
number on it is no better than no record.

The number on a record is a **copy taken at signature**, not a link to your account.
Changing your profile later does not alter anything already signed, and it should not.

That is also why **Certified by**, **Certificate** and **Type** on the sign-off form are all
editable. An outside IA who signed your annual has no account here; type their name, their
number and their rating, and the record says who actually certified the work rather than
who typed it in.

## Hand it to an inspector

<Where app="web">Reports → Airworthiness compliance</Where> is the same information as a
report: filter it to one aircraft, group it, add the method-of-compliance column, and
export.

<Callout type="note">
The report covers the date range you choose, so it is a history of a **period**, not a
complete AD status. A rule complied with before the window will not appear in it. Set the
range wide enough to cover everything the inspector is asking about.
</Callout>

Technicians can open this report. It is filed under fleet reports rather than compliance
reports for exactly that reason.

## What this does not do yet

Worth knowing before you rely on it:

- **It does not tell you which ADs apply to your aircraft.** You add the ones that apply;
  there is no catalogue that proposes them from your make, model and serial.
- **Component ADs stay with the airframe.** An engine AD is tracked against the aircraft,
  so it does not follow the engine through an overhaul swap.
- **"Due at the next annual" cannot be expressed.** Use the interval the AD gives in hours
  or months.
- **A one-off AD takes a date, not an hours deadline.** For "comply within the next 50
  hours", track it as a recurring hour interval and stop tracking it once done.
