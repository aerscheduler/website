<Summary>
With **Find the ADs for our aircraft** on, each aircraft has an **ADs** tab listing the Airworthiness
Directives published since 1995 that name its make and model, its engine or its propeller. Read
each one and mark it **Applies** (and track it) or **Not applicable** with a reason. Nothing is
decided for you, and every decision keeps who made it and when.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins, Technicians</Roles>

<Callout type="important">
A listed AD is one worth reading, not one that applies. An AD's applicability names serial
ranges, installed parts and exceptions that only a person can check against the aircraft and its
records. ADs issued before 1995 are not in the list at all, so an older airframe's still have to
be found the way you find them today.
</Callout>

## Turn it on

An owner or admin turns on **Find the ADs for our aircraft** under <Where app="web">Settings → Airworthiness
Directives</Where>. See [Choose how much AerScheduler does about Airworthiness
Directives](/docs/maintenance/choose-how-we-handle-airworthiness-directives). The **ADs** tab then
appears on every aircraft, for owners, admins and technicians, and the aircraft's page in the app
gets an **ADs** row.

## What the list is

The AD library holds every AD the Federal Register has published since 1995, and checks for new
ones daily. An aircraft's list is the ADs in it that name:

- **Its airframe**, by make and model. A Cessna's ADs may name *Textron Aviation*, the company
  that holds the type certificate now; those are included.
- **Its engine**, by make and model, when the aircraft has one recorded. Looking the tail number
  up on the aircraft form fills the engine in from the FAA registry.
- **Its propeller**, when one is recorded. The registry does not carry it, so type it in.

The list is grouped by decision (**To review**, **Applies**, **Not applicable**), and inside
each by what the AD is written against: **Airframe**, **Engine** or **Propeller**. Each row says
how the AD names the aircraft: **names 172S** (the exact model), **in the range
172A through 172S** (a letter range that covers it), or **names the 172 series** (its family,
which you need to read closely). An AD replaced by a later one is left off; the later one says
what it supersedes.

## Decide each one

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open the aircraft and choose ADs">
    Choose a row to read the AD beside the list: who it applies to, a short summary, and what it
    requires, laid out as the Federal Register prints it, with numbered paragraphs, notes and
    serial-number tables. What matched is highlighted, and a link opens the full AD.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Choose Decide">
    **Decide** is on each row and at the bottom of the AD you are reading.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="If it applies, choose Applies">
    **Track it as an inspection** opens Add inspections with the AD filled in: set its interval
    or deadline and save, and it is tracked like any AD, with its compliance record when signed
    off. **Applies, nothing to track** records that it applies when there is nothing left to
    schedule, such as a one-time AD already complied with.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="If it does not apply, choose Not applicable">
    Pick why: the serial number is outside the range, the affected part is not installed, an
    earlier action terminated it, or other (say why in the note). The note is the place for what
    you checked, such as the serial number.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Decided ADs move to **Applies** or **Not applicable**, each showing who decided and when. An AD
already tracked by an inspection with its number shows under **Applies** without anyone deciding
it again. To change a decision, open the row's **…** menu, or **Change the decision** on the AD,
and choose **Back to review**. Every decision is in the audit log.

## On your phone

Open the aircraft in the app and choose <Where app="mobile">ADs</Where>, under its inspections.
The list has the same groups. Tap an AD to read it, then use the gear menu to mark it **Applies:
track it**, **Applies: nothing to track** or **Not applicable** with a reason, or to send it
**Back to review**. Decisions made on the phone and on the web are the same decisions.

## When the list is short or empty

- **No engine or propeller recorded.** Their ADs are not listed. An owner or admin can add them
  with **Edit aircraft**.
- **No serial number.** The list is the same, but you need the serial to rule an AD out on its
  range.
- **The library is still loading.** The first import takes a few hours after this feature
  arrives; the tab says so, and the list grows as it fills.
