<Roles>Aircraft owners</Roles>

## Get in

The shop emails you how to sign in, or tells you its code. Create an AerScheduler account with
**the address the shop has for you** (the one its emails come to), confirm it, and join with the
shop's code. Your aircraft are there when you arrive, whatever name you sign up under.

<Callout type="warning">
Sign up with the address the shop writes to. With any other address the shop's record of you stays
theirs, and you join with nothing to see. If the shop recorded two people at one address (a
family inbox, say), sign up with the name the shop has for you so it can tell you apart.
</Callout>

You see your own aircraft, their jobs and your bills, and nothing else of the organization's:
not its schedule, its people or its fleet.

If you also fly or work there, your **Home** page is your usual one, with **Your aircraft** and
**Needs you** added below your day, and **Request work** under **Quick actions**.

## Your home page

If you only own an aircraft here, the figures across the top are yours: how many aircraft you
have here and how many are in the shop, how many things need you, what you owe, and the next
inspection due.

**Your aircraft** lists each one on a line: where its job stands, the date the shop promised it
back, which job has findings waiting on your answer, and the next inspection coming due, in
orange when it is close or overdue. Click a line to open the aircraft.

**Needs you** lists what is waiting on you, each with the button that does it: findings to
answer (**Review**, one line for each job that has them), an invoice to pay (**View and pay**),
and times to update when the shop's last reading is more than a month old (**Update times**).
When it is empty, nothing needs you.

## Answer what the shop found

When the shop finds something while working on your aircraft, they send it to you when it is
written up: you get an email (and a notification in AerScheduler once you have signed in), and
the job shows it at the top of **The work**, under **Waiting on your answer**, with an estimate
when they have priced it. Every owner of the aircraft is told; only the person the job is billed
to sees the prices.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Open the job">
    On your home page, click **Review** beside the finding under **Needs you**. Or open the
    aircraft from **Your aircraft** and pick the job under **Work with the shop**.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Approve, Decline or Later">
    **Approve** tells the shop to go ahead. **Decline** tells them not to. **Later** puts it off
    to another visit. When several are waiting, **Approve all** approves them together and
    shows the estimated total first. The shop sees your answer on the job straight away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Once you have answered, the answer stands: the shop may already have ordered the part. To
change it, call the shop. Once the shop has raised the invoice, you answer by calling them too.

If the shop rewords a finding while you have the job open, your answer is not taken: the page
says the finding changed and reloads, and you answer what it says once the shop sends it to you
again. Your answer is not taken either if the shop raises the invoice in the meantime.

Each answer says who gave it: **You approved** when you answered here, or **Approved**,
**Declined** or **Later** when the shop took your answer on the phone or a co-owner gave it.

## Ask for work

Click **Request work** under **Quick actions** on your home page (it asks which aircraft when
you have more than one), in the **+** menu at the top right of any page, or on the aircraft's
page. Say what you would like done, and when you would like
it back if it matters. The shop sees it on their board as a request and gets in touch to
schedule it.

## Keep your times current

The shop works out inspections due by hours from the last Hobbs and tach reading it has. After
you fly, click **Update times** under **Quick actions**, or on the aircraft's page. It is there
for an aircraft the shop looks after for you; an aircraft the organization flies (a leaseback)
has its times recorded at every flight instead. The page says how long ago the times
were last read.

A reading lower than the one on record asks why: the last reading was mistyped, or the meter
was replaced. Only a replaced meter changes how the aircraft's total time is counted. A reading that reaches an inspection the aircraft must have before it
flies asks too, because saving it takes the aircraft off the line until the shop signs the
inspection off.

## See the charges and pay

**Charges so far** on a job lists the labor and parts as the shop enters them, before tax and
fees: the work you asked for and the findings you approved. A finding still waiting on your
answer shows its estimate beside it instead, and one you declined or put off is not counted.
When the shop raises the invoice, the job shows it: click **View and pay**, or open
<Where app="web">Invoices</Where>. From then on **Charges so far** lists exactly what the invoice
charges, before tax and fees, and nothing waits on your answer: talk to the shop about anything
on it you did not agree to.

If you co-own an aircraft, you see the work on every job, but the charges and the invoice only
on the jobs billed to you. Any owner can answer what the shop found, and the shop sees who did.

If the shop bills you for a job on an aircraft you do not own (one you have since sold, for
example), it still shows on your home page with that job and its bill, but not the aircraft's
inspections or times.

## What you hear about

Once you have signed in, these land in your notifications and, by your settings, your email:

- what the shop finds and sends you;
- when your aircraft is booked in, arrives at the shop, is ready for pickup, or its promised date
  moves;
- inspections coming due: 30 and 7 days before, when due, and overdue; 10 hours before and when
  due for one counted in hours. Several at once come in one message, the most urgent first. Not
  while the inspection is on a job the shop has booked in, since you know about it already;
- invoices and payment reminders.

Choose which reach your email under <Where app="web">your organization's name → Notification settings</Where>,
in **Your aircraft** and **Billing**. Before you sign up, the shop's messages come by email to the
address they have for you, with a way to sign up, and each has an **Unsubscribe** link that works
without signing in.

## Stop signing in to a shop

Click **Leave organization** on your profile's Security tab. You stop seeing the shop, and the
shop keeps its record of you, your aircraft and your bills, as it had before you signed up.
Your saved cards and autopay do not stay with it: they are removed when you leave, so nothing
you saved can be charged again. If you sign in to the shop again later, add a card again.

## Photos and documents

The shop can share photos and paperwork with you on a job, under **Photos and documents**.
Click **Attach** to add your own, up to five at a time.
