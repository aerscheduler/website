## Open My training

Sign in to the web console and look under **You** in the left navigation for **My training**.
It has two tabs: **Progress** and **Endorsements**.

You will not see the school's Training page, and you cannot open anyone else's record. This
page is your own record only, and it shows exactly the same numbers your instructor and your
school see. That is deliberate: §141.101 makes the record yours as much as theirs.

<Callout type="note">
My training appears in the navigation for people carrying the student or instructor role. If
you rent rather than train and your school has put you on a course, ask them to check your
role so the item appears.
</Callout>

## Read your progress

One card per course you are enrolled on, showing the course, which revision of the syllabus
you are training against, the date you enrolled, and your status.

Then two measures, and they are not the same thing:

- **Lessons**, as a bar. How far through the syllabus you are.
- **Requirements**, one row each. The hours or the landings you must accumulate before you can
  test.

Neither predicts the other. You can be on lesson 19 of 21 and still be hours short, or have
the hours long before the syllabus is finished.

A lesson only counts as complete once your instructor has signed it with a passing mark. A
lesson you have to fly again stays on your record and does not move the bar.

## Why a requirement shows fewer hours than you flew

Because that requirement has a rule limiting what counts, and the page will say which one:

- Time in a simulator or training device, above what the course allows toward that
  requirement.
- Training transferred in from another school, above the limit for previous training.
- Training that has fallen outside the requirement's recency window, when it has to have been
  flown within the last few calendar months.

<Callout type="important">
None of your flying has been lost. Every hour is on your record, and the hours actually flown
are shown beside the credited figure. The requirement is only counting the part of it the
rule allows.
</Callout>

If a number still looks wrong, ask your instructor rather than assuming. They can see the same
ledger you can, entry by entry.

## Sign a lesson your instructor signed

When your instructor signs a lesson, it waits for you to acknowledge it. On the Progress tab a
highlighted block reads that a lesson needs your signature. Click **Sign** beside it.

Signing does not change the grade or the hours: those were credited when your instructor
signed. Your signature completes the record.

Two things you may see instead:

- Your instructor has not signed the lesson yet, so there is nothing to countersign.
- You have already signed it.

<Callout type="note">
Nothing chases an unsigned lesson, and nothing is blocked while one is outstanding. It is
still worth clearing them: a chief instructor reading the training records report before an
inspection will see every record you have not countersigned.
</Callout>

## Your endorsements

The **Endorsements** tab lists the endorsements signed for you, with their expiry state. A
solo endorsement carries a 90 day clock, and this is where you check it before you plan a
solo flight.

Only an instructor or an administrator can sign or renew one. If yours is close to expiring,
ask your instructor to renew it, which signs a fresh endorsement rather than extending the
old one.

## Doing all of this on the app

Open **Training** from the app menu. Your list is headed by the course, with the revision
underneath. Tap it.


The enrollment screen carries the same lessons bar, how many lessons of how many are
complete, your course fee if there is one, an Up next card for the lesson you are heading
for, and two tabs: **Lessons** and **Requirements**. A signed lesson shows its grade and its
hours on the row.

If a lesson is waiting for you, a **Waiting for your signature** card appears near the top.
Tap **Sign** on it. This is the same action as on the web, and it is the only write you have
in the training module.
