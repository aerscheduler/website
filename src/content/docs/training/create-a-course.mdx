## Decide Part 61 or Part 141 first

**Trained under** is the only field on a course that can never be edited afterwards, by
anyone, on any screen. Settle it before you type a name.

Templates are always created as **Part 61**. If you are running an approved Part 141
course, use **New course**, pick Part 141, and build the syllabus yourself. See
[Part 61 vs Part 141](/docs/training/part-61-vs-part-141).

## What is in each template

| Template | Notes |
|---|---|
| Private Pilot, Airplane Single-Engine Land | The requirement list has been checked line by line against §61.109(a). |
| Instrument Rating, Airplane (Part 61) | Check the requirement list against the CFR before you rely on it. |
| Commercial Pilot, Single-Engine (Part 61) | Check the requirement list against the CFR before you rely on it. |
| Flight Instructor, Airplane Single-Engine (Part 61) | Check the requirement list against the CFR before you rely on it. |

Each one arrives with every stage, lesson, ACS-coded task and hour requirement already in
place, the **Credits toward** map wired up so hours actually accumulate, and the grading
scale set to S, U and I.

<Callout type="warning">
Only the Private Pilot template's requirements have been verified against the regulation.
The other three look equally authoritative in the picker and are a starting point, not a
compliance document. Read them against the CFR before a student's graduation depends on
them.
</Callout>

## What you get: a draft called Rev A

Either route leaves you with one course holding one **draft** version labelled Rev A.
Nothing is published, so nothing is locked and everything is editable.

The header badge marks the version **Draft** and safe to edit while that is true. There is
no version dropdown in the header yet: it appears only once a course has more than one
version, so everything in the Syllabus, Requirements and Students sections is Rev A.

## What the templates will not do for you

- **They are Part 61.** They cannot be converted.
- **The lesson sequence is not from any regulation.** It is a sensible order, not an
  approved one. Expect to change it.
- **They are not an approved Training Course Outline.** Approval is per school and per
  FSDO.
- **They do not know your fleet, your rates or your local airspace.** Objectives and
  completion standards are generic, and worth a read before your first student flies them.

<Callout type="tip">
Forking a template and editing it down is usually faster than starting empty, even for a
Part 141 school that has to rebuild in a Part 141 course. Fork one, read the lesson list,
then copy what you want into your own course.
</Callout>
