<Summary>
Enroll the student first, then open their training record, go to Requirements, and click
**Add credit**. Choose the requirement, say where the time came from, enter the hours, and
set **When it was flown** to the date in the logbook rather than today.
</Summary>

<Roles>Owners, Admins</Roles>

Instructors can do this too if they hold the Enroll and graduate grant. See
[Who can see and do what in Training](/docs/training/who-can-do-what-in-training).

## Post a credit

<Where app="web">Training → Students → the student → Requirements</Where>

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Enroll the student first">
    Credit is posted against a requirement, and requirements come from the course version the
    student is enrolled on. There is nowhere to park hours before an enrollment exists.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Click Add credit">
    On the Requirements tab of their training record.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Choose the requirement">
    One credit feeds one requirement. Time that counts toward three requirements needs three
    credits.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Enter the amount">
    Hours for an hours requirement, or How many for one measured in events, such as night
    landings.
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="Set When it was flown, and add a note">
    The date from the logbook. A note such as the previous school's name and that you have
    seen the logbook is what makes this defensible later.
  </Step>
  <Step n={6} title="Click Post credit">
    The Requirements bars move immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Choose where it came from

Four sources, and the choice decides which ceiling applies to the hours.

| Where it came from | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Previous training (Part 61) | Training done elsewhere under Part 61, from the student's logbook. |
| Previous training (Part 141) | Training done at another Part 141 or Part 142 school. |
| Simulator or training device | Device time, which the course may cap separately. |
| Correction by hand | A number you are putting right yourself. A note is required. |

## The date is the point

**When it was flown** looks optional beside the requirement and the hours. It is not.

If you leave it blank it defaults to today. On any requirement that only counts training
from the last few calendar months, that quietly makes a 2019 logbook look like it was flown
this morning, and the student is told they are ready when they are not.

<Callout type="warning">
Enter the real date every time, even on requirements that have no recency window. You cannot
tell from the Add credit dialog which requirements have one.
</Callout>

A date in the future is refused: training cannot be credited before it has happened. Zero is
refused, and so is a negative number.

## Why some of it may not count

Post 30 hours and the requirement may still show fewer. That is the course's own ceilings
doing their job, not the credit failing.

A requirement can cap how much of it may be met by previous training, and how much by device
time. The caps exist because §141.77 limits transferred credit, and Appendix B limits
simulator time. They are applied when the record is read, so every hour you posted stays on
the ledger; the requirement simply refuses to count more than its share.

The Requirements row says which one bit. See
[Read a training record](/docs/training/read-a-training-record).

## Credits you cannot take back

There is no button anywhere in the console to reverse a credit you posted by hand. Check the
requirement, the amount and the date before you click Post credit.

The one undo the console does have is [amending a signed lesson
record](/docs/training/correct-a-signed-lesson), which reverses the credits that lesson
posted. That does nothing for a hand-posted transfer or simulator credit.

## Read the result on the Ledger

<Where app="web">Training → Students → the student → Ledger</Where>

Every credit and every reversal, with its amount, the requirement it fed, a chip saying where
it came from, and its date. Nothing is stored as a running total: a student's standing is
the sum of these rows, which is why "why does this read 41.2 hours?" is still answerable next
year.
