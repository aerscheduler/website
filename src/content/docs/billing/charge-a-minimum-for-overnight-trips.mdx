## Set the minimum

<Steps>
<Step n={1} title="Open Settings and choose Billing">
</Step>
<Step n={2} title="Enter a figure in Overnight minimum (hrs/night)">
In decimal hours, so 2.0 or 1.5. Leaving it blank means no minimum, which is the default.
</Step>
<Step n={3} title="Optionally set Return grace after midnight (minutes)">
Landing within this many minutes after local midnight still counts as the same night, not
another one. Blank or 0 means no grace: landing even a minute after midnight starts a new
night, exactly as before this setting existed.
</Step>
<Step n={4} title="Click Save changes">
Only the organization owner can save this card. An admin can read it but the save will be
refused.
</Step>
</Steps>

It takes effect on the next close-out. Invoices already raised never change.

## How nights are counted

A night is a local midnight the booking crosses, counted in the **airport's** time zone
rather than whatever zone the person closing out happens to be in.

| Booking | Nights |
|---|---|
| Out Saturday morning, back Saturday evening | 0 |
| Out Friday, back Saturday | 1 |
| Out Friday, back Sunday | 2 |

A booking that comes back the same day is never affected, however long it ran.

<Callout type="note">
With grace set, a landing inside that window after midnight still counts toward the
night that just ended. Out Friday, back a few minutes after midnight Sunday with 15
minutes of grace still counts as 1 night, not 2.
</Callout>

## It is a floor, never a reduction

The minimum only ever raises a bill. AerScheduler compares the time actually flown against
nights multiplied by the minimum, and bills whichever is larger.

- Two nights, minimum 2.0, 1.5 hours flown: the aircraft time bills as **4.0 hours**.
- Two nights, minimum 2.0, 6.0 hours flown: the aircraft time bills as **6.0 hours**, the
  minimum does nothing.

## One minimum per booking, not per person

The minimum is applied to the booking before the cost is split, so it is one minimum
between everybody on the trip.

<Callout type="important">
Two pilots sharing a weekend away owe one minimum between them, not one each. Whatever
your [cost-splitting rule](/docs/billing/set-up-cost-splitting) is for that booking type
then divides the raised total the same way it divides everything else.
</Callout>

## What the pilot sees at ramp-in

Nobody should be surprised by this on an invoice. When a booking crosses a night and a
minimum is set, a notice appears on the booking at every close-out step before invoicing,
and the ramp-in dialog spells it out in figures before you save: away two nights, the
school's minimum is 2.0 hours a night, so this will bill 4.0 hours rather than the 1.5
flown.

<Screenshot id="close-out-overnight-notice" />

If the flown time is already above the minimum, no notice appears, because nothing changes.

## You cannot exempt a single aircraft

There is nowhere in the web console or the iOS app to give one tail its own overnight
minimum, or to exclude it. The figure you set here applies to every aircraft in the fleet,
so pick one that is fair across the fleet, or leave it blank and handle long trips by hand.
