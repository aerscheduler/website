<Summary>
The type is the first field on the booking form and it decides everything under it: what
kind of resource is booked, who may be on it, how many people fit, and who gets the
invoice. Picking the wrong one is the most common reason a booking is refused.
</Summary>

## The eight types at a glance

| Type | What it books | Who is on it |
|---|---|---|
| Dual | Aircraft (or nothing) | 1 instructor and up to 4 students |
| Solo | Aircraft | Exactly 1 pilot |
| Shared flight | Aircraft | 2 or more pilots, no instructor. Up to 4 students and 4 renters |
| Rental | Aircraft | Up to 4 renters |
| Guest | Aircraft | 1 guest, plus an instructor if you want one |
| Ground | Room | Up to 2 instructors and 12 students |
| Sim | Simulator | Up to 2 instructors and 6 students |
| Maintenance | Aircraft | Nobody at all |

## Dual, solo and shared flight

These three are where most of the confusion lives, and the difference is not stylistic.

**Dual** is an instructor flying with a student. It is also the only type the booking form
accepts with no aircraft at all, which is how you book a briefing or a ground session in
no particular room.

**Solo** is one pilot alone. Adding a second person is refused:

> A solo has one pilot. Book a dual if an instructor is flying with a student.

That is not a product limitation. 14 CFR 61.87 defines solo time as time when the
student is the sole occupant, so a two-person solo would be a false record.

**Shared flight** means two or more pilots sharing one aircraft with no instructor: a
cross-country split between two renters, or safety-pilot work under 91.109. It is
refused both ways round:

> A shared flight is two or more pilots sharing one aircraft. With one pilot, book a solo or a rental instead.

> A shared flight has no instructor. With an instructor aboard it's a dual.

<Callout type="warning">
The iOS app has no Shared flight type at all. Its type buttons are Solo, Dual, Ground,
Guest, Simulator, Rental and Maintenance. Book a shared flight from the web console.
</Callout>

A shared flight raises one invoice per payer, not one invoice with shares inside it. See
[Split a flight between two or more people](/docs/scheduling/split-a-booking-between-payers).

## Rental and guest flights

**Rental** is a renter taking an aircraft with no instruction. It takes renters only, so
no instructor field is offered. When a renter books one themselves, they see the aircraft
they are checked out on.

**Guest** is a discovery or introductory flight for somebody who has no account. It needs
an aircraft, and it needs the guest's email address, because that is where the invoice
goes:

> Enter a valid email. The guest's invoice is sent there.

A guest never signs anything. An admin or the instructor on the flight closes it out with
**Close out and bill guest**, and the guest is emailed a link to pay by card. See
[Close out and bill a discovery or guest flight](/docs/scheduling/close-out-a-guest-flight).

## Ground and sim sessions

**Ground** books a room. **Sim** books a simulator. The resource field on the form
relabels itself to match, so if you are looking for the aircraft picker and finding a
Room picker, check the type.

A Ground lesson never ramps out. Nothing has a Hobbs meter in a classroom, so the
close-out button reads **Review times** and instruction time is the only figure recorded.
See [Close out a ground lesson](/docs/scheduling/close-out-a-ground-lesson).

A Sim session is not the same. A simulator carries meters, so a sim booking gets the
normal **Ramp out** and **Ramp in** buttons and asks for Hobbs and tach readings, exactly
like a flight.

<Callout type="note">
A booking with no resource on it at all, such as a briefing booked as a dual with no
aircraft, also gets the short **Review times** close-out. There is nothing to read.
</Callout>

## Maintenance

Maintenance blocks the aircraft for the whole window and carries nobody at all. Trying to
put a person on one is refused:

> A maintenance booking can't have anyone assigned to it. It takes the aircraft off the line.

Maintenance is also the one exception to grounding. A grounded aircraft can be booked for
maintenance and nothing else, because the booking exists to get it back in service.

Because a maintenance booking has no personnel, a technician's own maintenance still
appears on their <Where app="web">You &rarr; Schedule</Where> page: it is matched by who
created it.

## Which types your role can create

| Role | Types offered |
|---|---|
| Owner, admin, dispatcher | All eight |
| Instructor | Dual, Solo, Shared flight, Ground, Sim, Guest |
| Student | Dual, Solo, Shared flight, Ground, Sim |
| Renter | Rental, Shared flight |
| Technician | Maintenance |

## Why changing the type clears the resource

Each type books exactly one kind of resource. Switch from Sim to Dual and the simulator
you had picked is no longer a legal choice, so the field empties.

That is the form protecting you, not losing your work. Pick the type first, then the
resource, and it will not happen.
