<Summary>
Your whole schedule is one page. The **Calendar** link in the left nav opens a board
titled **The Ramp**, which shows every booking your school has for the day, week or
month you are looking at. Each booking then walks the same path: booked, ramped out,
ramped in, signed off, invoiced.
</Summary>

## The board, and what it shows

<Where app="web">Calendar</Where> opens <Where app="web">The Ramp</Where> at
`/schedule`. It is the whole organization's schedule, not just yours. Every member can
see it, on purpose, so nobody books over a flight they could not see.

What changes by role is which lanes are drawn and what you can act on:

- Owners, admins, dispatchers, instructors and students see aircraft, simulator and room lanes.
- Renters and technicians see aircraft lanes only.

A red now-line is drawn across today so you can see at a glance what is out. The
counter on the right reads something like `47 reservations`, and switches to
`12 of 47 matching` once you start filtering.

<Screenshot id="schedule-day-board" />

<Callout type="note">
The board refreshes itself about every 20 seconds, so bookings appear and change
without you doing anything. It skips the refresh while somebody is mid-drag, because
pulling the block out from under the cursor would be worse.
</Callout>

Cancelled bookings are not drawn on the board at all. To find one, use the
[Cancellations report](/docs/scheduling/cancel-a-reservation) or open the booking's own
link. The iOS app's Reservations list does include them, under its Canceled filter.

## The life of a booking

Every booking carries a status badge in its detail panel. The badge is the fastest way
to see where a flight is stuck.

| Badge | What has happened | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| Not started | It is booked. The aircraft is still on the line. | [Ramp out](/docs/scheduling/ramp-out-and-ramp-in) when it leaves. |
| In flight | Ramped out. The departure Hobbs and tach are recorded. | Ramp in when the aircraft is back. |
| Awaiting review | The return readings are in. | Every pilot on it [signs off with their PIN](/docs/scheduling/sign-off-with-your-pin). |
| Reviewed | Everyone has signed off. | The invoice is raised automatically. |
| Billed | The invoice exists. | The payer pays it. |
| Paid | Settled. | Nothing. |

A booking with no aircraft, such as a ground lesson in a classroom, skips the ramp
steps entirely. See [Close out a ground lesson](/docs/scheduling/close-out-a-ground-lesson).

<Screenshot id="reservation-detail-panel" />

The detail panel also carries a timeline of what happened to the booking: Booked,
Ramped out, Ramped in, Signed off, Invoiced, Paid, Cancelled, Last edited.

## Day, week, month and list

Four ways to look at the same bookings.

| View | What it draws |
|---|---|
| Day | Rows are resources (aircraft, simulators, rooms), columns are hours. |
| Week | Seven columns, one per day, with a time grid down the side. |
| Month | A grid of whole weeks, up to three bookings per day, then a `+N more` link. |
| List | The same bookings as a plain vertical list instead of a grid. |

Use the Month / Week / Day tabs to switch, the arrows either side to step forward and
back, and **Today** to come home. There is a date picker in the header if you need to
jump somewhere specific.

The Board / List toggle is a desktop control. On a narrow screen you always get the
list, because a resource grid on a phone is unreadable.

## Rows are resources, and one row is the catch-all

On the day board every row is a thing you can book: a tail number, a simulator, a room.
The row you click is what the new booking is for. Clicking a room lane opens the form
already set to Ground and a simulator lane already set to Sim. Clicking an aircraft lane
leaves the type alone, because six of the eight types book an aircraft.

The last row is the catch-all. It is labelled **Unassigned** when it holds bookings with
no resource at all, and **Other** when it also holds bookings whose lane is not drawn on
your board, such as a room booking seen by a renter.

<Screenshot id="schedule-unassigned-row" />

<Callout type="tip">
Nothing is ever dropped off the board. If a booking is not in a lane, it is in this row.
Dual is the one type that can be booked with no aircraft, which is how you schedule a
briefing, and those bookings live here.
</Callout>

## Where to find your own bookings

The board is the whole school. For just your own flights:

- <Where app="web">You &rarr; Schedule</Where> lists the bookings you are on, grouped by Today, Tomorrow and then by weekday. A technician sees the maintenance they scheduled.
- <Where app="web">You &rarr; Home</Where> shows your next reservation and the ones coming up.

Opening a booking from either place gives you the same detail panel as the board, so you
can ramp out, ramp in and sign off from there.

<Screenshot id="my-schedule-list" />

## What the iOS app does

The app is the same account, narrowed to what a pilot needs away from a desk: the
calendar, your reservations, creating a booking, and the view-reservation screen with its
progress stepper (Ramp out, Ramp in, Review times, Completed).

Correcting a reading after the fact and overriding a booking's rates are on both surfaces.
The app calls them **Update Details** and **Override Payment**; the console calls them
**Correct times** and **Override payment**, on the reservation panel.
