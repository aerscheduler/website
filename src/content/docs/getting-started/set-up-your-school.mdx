## Before you start

Nothing. No credit card and no sales call. A 14-day free trial starts the moment you sign
up.

Whoever creates the operation becomes its **owner** and **admin** automatically.

## Create the account

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Go to app.aerscheduler.com and create your account">
    Name, work email and a password, or sign up with Google or Apple.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Open the verification email and click the link">
    Until you do, every page sends you back to the verification screen. It checks every
    few seconds and moves on by itself once you click the link, so you do not need to
    press anything. There is a resend button if it never arrives.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Say what you are here for">
    Joining a school someone else runs is a different path: you just need their code. See
    [Join a school with a code](/docs/getting-started/join-a-school). Otherwise you are
    setting up your own operation, and AerScheduler walks you through it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What setup asks for

Three wizard steps, plus a short question about where to start.

**Your operation.** Whether you are a flight school, a flying club or a rental operation,
what it is called, and the airport you fly from. That airport becomes your first location,
and every aircraft, simulator and room belongs to one.

You also pick **what you want working first** (schedule, maintenance, billing, training, or
reports). That choice puts those jobs under **Start here** on the dashboard checklist; the
rest stays under **Also set up**. If you arrived from a marketing page about one of those
topics, that option is usually pre-selected.

If AerScheduler cannot tell how you found the product (no ad click, no clear referrer),
it may also ask **how you heard about us**. That is optional and only used so we know
what is working.

**Your first aircraft.** Tail number, year, current Hobbs and tach, and an hourly rate.
You can skip it and add aircraft later from
[Add an aircraft](/docs/getting-started/add-an-aircraft).

**Billing.** Connecting Stripe is what turns a closed-out flight into a bill your members
can settle: either a Stripe invoice per visit, or (if you choose Account ledger) a charge
against a prepaid balance. You can do it later without blocking anything else. See
[Turn on invoicing and card payments](/docs/billing/turn-on-invoicing-and-card-payments)
and [Choose invoice or account ledger](/docs/billing/choose-invoice-or-account-ledger).

Once you are through, your dashboard shows what is still missing and links straight to
each job. The list leads with the area you said you care about first under Start here. It
works that out from your real data rather than from a list of steps you ticked, so adding
an aircraft from anywhere in the product counts.

<Callout type="warning">
Set your school's time zone early, under <Where app="web">Settings → Organization</Where>.
Until it is set, two people can see different times for the same booking, and multi-day
bookings cannot be enabled at all. See
[Why the schedule shows airport time](/docs/scheduling/airport-time-and-time-zones).
</Callout>

## A sensible first week

Setup gets you bookable. This is the order that gets you *useful*, unless your checklist
already put a different module first.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Get your fleet in">
    Aircraft first, then simulators and classrooms. Rates and meters matter here, because
    billing and maintenance both read them.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Set your instruction rates">
    A lesson cannot be priced until its instruction type has a rate.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Invite your instructors, then your students">
    Roles decide what each person sees and can book. See
    [Roles, and what each one can do](/docs/getting-started/roles-and-permissions).
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Decide your booking rules">
    Who may book what, and whether members need a card on file. See
    [Booking rules and settings](/docs/scheduling/booking-rules-and-settings).
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="Put maintenance due dates in">
    So the schedule knows when a tail should stop flying. See
    [How maintenance tracking works](/docs/maintenance/how-maintenance-tracking-works).
  </Step>
  <Step n={6} title="Book a real flight and close it out">
    One end-to-end flight tells you more about whether your rates and roles are right than
    any amount of settings review.
  </Step>
</Steps>
