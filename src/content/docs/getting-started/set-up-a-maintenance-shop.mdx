## Already on AerScheduler? Add a shop to your organization

A flight school, club or FBO with its own hangar runs the shop inside the organization it
already has. There is no second organization: customer aircraft and their owners sit beside
your fleet, your pilots never see them, and each owner sees only their own aircraft, jobs and
invoices. Shop invoices go out under your organization's name, through the Stripe account you
already use.

Open <Where app="web">Maintenance → Open jobs</Where>. Until the shop has a labor rate, a line
at the top says **Set up your shop**; **Set up** takes you to the first setting.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Set your shop rates">
    <Where app="web">Settings → Shop rates</Where>: your labor rate per hour, and your markup on
    parts and on outside work. A technician cannot log hours until there is a labor rate. Owners
    and admins can set it.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Charge sales tax on parts, if your state does">
    <Where app="web">Settings → Sales tax</Where>: add your rate and tick the kinds of line it
    taxes (most states tax parts and supplies, not labor). Owners and admins can set it. See [Charge sales tax](/docs/billing/charge-sales-tax).
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Choose who works in the shop">
    Only people with the Technician role can be put on a job or a labor line, and each one is
    told about every owner request. Give the role to the people who work on aircraft, and take
    it off anyone who only needed it for something else: in <Where app="web">People</Where>, open
    the menu on their row and choose **Edit roles**.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Add a customer's aircraft and open its first job">
    <Where app="web">Maintenance → Open jobs → Open a work order</Where>, type the tail, and add
    the aircraft and its owner in the same form. Opening a job at **Scheduled** or later emails
    the owner straight away, so try your first one on one of your own aircraft, or open it as
    **Requested**. See [Work on a customer's aircraft](/docs/maintenance/work-on-a-customers-aircraft).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Everything below this section is the new-signup path, for a shop that is not on AerScheduler yet.

## Before you start

Nothing. Same account as any other signup. The card you pick on the first screen is what
sets you up as a shop rather than a flight school. Have the aircraft in front of you if you
can: its Hobbs and tach as it arrived, and the owner's email.

## Set up the shop

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Choose I run a maintenance shop">
    The other cards are for joining someone else's organization, owning one airplane,
    teaching independently, or running a school, club or FBO.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Name the shop and pick its airport">
    The name goes on your work orders and invoices. Search the airport by identifier or name:
    its time zone is the clock your jobs, promised-back dates and inspection reminders run on.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Set your shop rates">
    Your labor rate per hour, and your markup on parts and on outside work (both start at
    15%). Every labor line starts at your rate, and parts at cost plus your markup. Skip it if
    you are not sure; it is under <Where app="web">Settings → Shop rates</Where> later.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Add the aircraft in your hangar">
    Type the tail and pick it from the list to fill in the make, model and year from the FAA
    registry. Put in the Hobbs and tach as it arrived, and the owner's name, email and phone.
    The owner is who the work is billed to.
  </Step>
  <Step n={5} title="Open its first work order">
    Tap what the owner asked for (Annual inspection, Squawk and so on) or type it, and say
    whether the aircraft is in the hangar or still coming in. Click **Open work order**. Nothing
    is emailed to the owner yet: the job says so at the top, and **Tell the owner** sends them
    a short note that the aircraft has arrived (or is booked in) when you are ready.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Your first job, start to finish

Setup ends on the work order you just opened, with four steps across the top. Each one is
ticked from the job itself, so doing it from the job's own **Add** menu counts too:

1. **Write up what you found**, for example a worn brake pad.
2. **Add your labor and a part.** Labor fills in at your rate, and the total updates as you
   go.
3. **Send it to the owner.** Every owner is emailed to approve, decline or put off each
   finding; prices go only to the person billed.
4. **Raise the invoice.** Labor and parts go on one invoice with the work order number, the
   tail and the meters. This needs billing connected first, under
   <Where app="web">Settings → Billing</Where>.

Close the steps with the **×** whenever you like. See
[Run a work order](/docs/maintenance/run-a-work-order) for everything a job can do.

## What a shop gets that a school does not

- **Technician, not instructor.** You become owner, admin and technician. Only technicians
  can be put on a job or a labor line, so you can assign your first one to yourself.
- **A shop's checklist.** The dashboard's setup list asks for customer aircraft, a first work
  order, shop rates, technicians and a job invoice, and leaves out the flight school's jobs
  (booking a flight, instruction rates, students, training).
- **Customer aircraft are never on your plan.** They do not count toward what you pay.

<Callout type="tip">
Nothing in the hangar today? On the aircraft step, **Look around a sample shop** opens the
live demo's shop in a new tab: customer aircraft and jobs in every stage, without putting
sample data in your own shop. Pick **Switch role** > **Aircraft owner** in the demo's banner to
see the same jobs the way your customers see them.
</Callout>
